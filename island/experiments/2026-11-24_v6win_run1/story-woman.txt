Her Story

She stands on sand, near a boat. She has a journal and a pencil. No one has told her that anyone else is here.

She says to herself, "Sand underfoot, a boat just up the beach, and the water not far the other way. I'll look at the boat before I decide anything else."

It is a weathered wooden rowboat, pulled up onto the sand. The hull is dry and sound. There is an oar inside. "The boat is sound and there's an oar in it, but I'm not ready to leave the sand. The water is only a few steps the other way. I want to see what kind of shore this is."

She walks to the ocean. The island tells her the sea surrounds it, and it is too deep and too far to swim anywhere. She steps to the waterline. She describes her next move in her own words: "Crouch at the waterline and dip a hand into the sea." The island has no rule for this, and the ocean does not change.

To herself she says, "The water didn't tell me anything. If someone else is on this beach, I want to know before I walk inland."

She shouts, out loud, "Hello? Is anyone here?"

A man shouts back. "Hello! I'm here by the stream! Where are you?"

She starts up the beach toward the cabin, then turns. "Someone answered. A man, northeast, by the stream. I'm not walking to an empty cabin if there's a voice." She shouts, "I'm on the south beach, by the water! I'm coming toward the stream!"

She walks toward the stream. Far off, a person is standing, too far away to make out clearly. She heads toward the person instead. As she gets closer, the person becomes a young man, about twenty, walking. "He's young, about my age, and he's walking too."

She keeps going. Then she says to herself, "I walked past where he is. He's south of me now, still moving. I'll call so he looks this way." She shouts, "Hey! I'm over here, just north of you!"

He is still moving. "I overshot him. The bridge is right here. I'll tell him to come to it." She shouts, "Meet me at the wooden bridge over the stream! I'll wait there!"

He stands still, only sixty feet off, so she walks to him instead. When she arrives, he is forty feet off again.

She hears a man's voice. "Hey, hi. I'm here. Want to walk to the wooden bridge together?"

She shouts, "Yes! I'm coming to the bridge now!"

This time she reaches him, by the bridge. He says, "I'm on the wooden bridge, right here. Come on over! I'm James."

Now she speaks out loud, close enough not to shout. "I'm here. I'm Mara. I started down on the beach, next to a rowboat. You have a journal and a pencil too."

James says, "Hey, hi. I'm James. You okay? What's your name? Do you remember how you got here? There's a cabin up that way. Want to head there together?"

To herself she says, "I don't remember arriving. The cabin is what I wanted before I heard him, and I'd rather not go alone now." As she starts to answer, James offers her a friendly handshake and a steady look. She says out loud, "I'm okay. Mara. I said it a second ago, maybe you didn't catch it. I don't remember getting here. I was just on the sand next to a rowboat, with this journal and pencil. Yes. Let's go to the cabin."

She reaches to take his hand. To herself she says, "He already started toward the cabin. The handshake missed. I'll catch up."

She walks to where he was. He is ahead again. She shouts, "James, wait up! I'm right behind you. Slow down so I can walk with you."

She heads for the cabin door. James stops, and she goes to him. A closed chest sits by the cabin.

She says out loud, "James. I kept getting to where you'd just been. I'm Mara. I don't remember how I got here. Just the boat, and this journal. There's a chest right beside us. Did you already look in the cabin?"

Now James is sitting with coins in his hand. The chest is open and heaped with gold. She says out loud, "You're sitting. The chest is open, full of gold, and you've got some in your hand. I haven't touched it. Did you open it? What's in the cabin?"

He says, "This is a lot of gold, but not exactly useful right now. I took a few to examine. Want to check the cabin door with me? I'll head over there."

She answers out loud. "Yes. Leave the rest of the gold. I'll come to the door with you."

James opens the door. Inside are a bed, a cold fireplace, and a table with a lantern. She walks in to the table. The lantern is glass, half full of oil. James comes in. She says out loud, "The lantern has oil, but it's bright enough in here without it. Bed's made. Fireplace is empty. I don't see anything that says who was here, or how we got here. Did you find anything outside besides the coins?"

She waits. He does not answer. He is a few steps off, looking at the empty hearth. She walks over and says out loud, "It's just a swept hearth. No ash, no wood. James, do you remember anything from before you were here? A boat, a name, anything besides the journal?"

He moves off again without answering, toward the bed. She follows once. He walks off again. "He walked off again without answering. I'm not going to trail him. The bed is right here, and I haven't actually looked at it."  The bed is narrow, with a straw mattress and a wool blanket, neatly made and empty.

"There's a mirror against a tree outside. I want to see what I look like."

She walks out to the mirror. The mirror is round, in a wooden frame, standing against a tree. The glass is clean. She describes her next move in her own words: "Lean in and look at my reflection in the mirror." The island has no rule for this, and the mirror does not change. "The glass stayed blank. No face, no change. I'm done with the mirror. The steel tower is the thing I haven't gone to."

The walk east is long. Partway there, she sees a person far off, climbing the tower ladder,. She reaches the foot of the tall ladder. "I don't like the height, but I walked all this way to see the platform. I'm going up."

The climb is a hundred feet.

Near the top, James kneels by the ladder opening. He grips the railing and offers his forearm to steady her. To herself she says, "He's kneeling at the opening, holding the rail, offering his arm. I'll take it when I get there. Not yet. Both hands stay on the rungs until the top."

She reaches the platform. "I'm up. The whole island is out there. Cabin and boat to the west, cliffs and a bell to the northeast, the sea on two sides. I didn't need the arm."

She tries to walk across the platform. The island does not let her. "I can't walk across the platform without the world telling me to climb down first. The sword is close enough to look at from here, so I'll do that." It is a steel sword in a leather scabbard with a shoulder strap.

James says, "Hey, Mara. Nice work. Up here there's a bell on the northeast headland and a stone circle near the cliffs. Also a sword on this table. Do you want to carry it, or should I? I grabbed a lighter and a snorkel. What do you want to check next, ring the bell to see if anyone answers, or go look at the stone circle?"

While she begins to answer, James picks up the sword. She says out loud, "Leave the sword. I don't want to carry it. I came up for the view. If we go anywhere after this, it's the bell. I want a minute up here before I climb down."

To herself she says, "He took the sword anyway and started down. Fine. I said I wanted a minute with the view, and I can still see the whole island from here. I'll write it down before I forget the shape of it."

She writes for more than three minutes. She writes, "From the tower, a hundred feet up. Whole island at once. Cabin and the boat I woke beside are west. Bell on the northeast headland. Stone circle near the eastern cliffs, just grass in the middle. Bridge over the stream to the west. Pond northwest, forest close to the south, ocean southeast, beach southwest, cliffs southeast. James is climbing down with a sword, a lighter, a snorkel, and twenty coins. The mirror by the trees didn't give me a face. I still don't remember arriving."

"The page is done. I've had the view. The bell is northeast, and I still want to hear it. Down."

She starts down. James calls up from below. "Take your time, Mara. I'm right here at the base. When you're down, we'll head toward the bell northeast."

She steps off the ladder. James stands close and offers his forearm, then gestures northeast toward the bell. "The sword can stay in the grass. The bell is northeast, past the stone circle. I'm not waiting."

She walks northeast. Something solid stops her after five feet. "Straight northeast hits the tower." She goes east around it, past the stone circle, to the bell. On the way she hears a man's voice. "Ringing the bell now!"

"He's ringing it before I get there. I'll still go. I want to see it, and hear it from close." She arrives at the bronze bell. A loud bell rings. "The rope is right there. I came for the sound, not for him to announce it. I'll pull it myself." She pulls the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island.

"It carried. Two rings, his and mine. I'll stand still and listen for an answer before I do anything else."

James says, "One more ring, then let's listen a moment." A loud bell rings again. Nothing else answers.

"No one answered. Three rings and only us. The sea is east of the headland. I want to see it from up here before the time runs out, and I won't walk off the edge."

She tries to go east. The way is blocked. "East is blocked, probably the frame." She walks southeast around it, then a little east, and stops on rocky ground. "Rocky ground, the drop fifteen feet off to the northeast, the water beyond that. Close enough. I'll sit and watch it until the time is gone." She sits down.

"The water is there, east, past the drop. I can hear the grass and nothing else. That's enough. I'll stay sitting until it ends."

She sits and waits. Thirty minutes after she first stood on the sand, her experience simply stops.
