His Story

A young man stands on a hilltop. He has a journal and a pencil. No one has told him that anyone else is on the island.

He says to himself, "I'll start by heading to the stream. Water usually tells me about a place."

He walks to the stream. It is clear and rocky, about ten feet wide, with small cascades.

"I want to feel the current and wake up a bit, then I'll head for that ladder to see the view."

He does it. In his own words: "Crouch in the stream, put my hand in to feel the temperature and current, splash some water on my face, and take a cautious sip."

At that moment he hears a woman shout: "Hello? Is anyone here?"

He starts walking to the tall metal ladder. Then he says to himself, "Someone else? I'll call back and head that way if she answers."

He shouts. These are the first words he says out loud on the island: "Hello! I'm here by the stream! Where are you?"

To himself he says, "Sounds southwest, farther than the cabin. I'll head that way via the cabin and call again once I'm closer." He runs toward the cabin door.

He hears her shout: "I'm on the south beach, by the water! I'm coming toward the stream!"

"Changing course to the wooden bridge to meet her faster." He runs to the bridge. He sees a person far off, too far away to make out clearly.

He walks toward the person. At a hundred and forty-eight feet he can make her out. She is a young woman, about twenty, and she is walking.

He hears her shout: "Hey! I'm over here, just north of you!"

When he gets where he was going, she is a hundred and fifty-six feet away, standing. He runs toward her. He hears her shout: "Meet me at the wooden bridge over the stream! I'll wait there!"

"On it, heading to the wooden bridge." He runs back to the bridge.

"She's right here, no need to sprint past her." He says out loud, "Hey, hi. I'm here. Want to walk to the wooden bridge together?"

He hears her shout, "Yes! I'm coming to the bridge now!"

He says out loud, "I'm on the wooden bridge, right here. Come on over! I'm James."

To himself he says, "I'm glad you found me." He starts to speak. While he speaks, he hears her voice. "I'm here. I'm Mara. I started down on the beach, next to a rowboat. You have a journal and a pencil too."

What he says out loud comes out in full: "Hey, hi. I'm James. You okay? What's your name? Do you remember how you got here? There's a cabin up that way, want to head there together?"

Then he says to himself, "Nice to meet you, Mara. I've got the same journal and pencil. I started up on the hill. You okay? Want to head to the cabin together? There's a chest by the door I'm curious about." Mara does not hear this.

He does something. In his own words: "Offer her a friendly handshake and a steady look to check she's okay before we walk."

Then he walks toward the wooden chest by the cabin. To himself he says, "Glad you're here, Mara. Let's check that chest by the cabin, maybe there's something useful."

As he walks, he hears Mara say, "I'm okay. Mara. I said it a second ago, maybe you didn't catch it. I don't remember getting here. I was just on the sand next to a rowboat, with this journal and pencil. Yes. Let's go to the cabin." He also hears a faint voice nearby. He cannot make out the words.

To himself he says, "Almost there. I thought I heard another voice, did you hear that?"

He hears Mara shout: "James, wait up! I'm right behind you. Slow down so I can walk with you."

He arrives at the chest, heavy and wooden, beside the cabin door. To himself he says, "Sorry, here by the chest. I'll wait; take your time coming up." He sits down beside it and waits.

"Hey, Mara. You made it. Let's open this together." He says this only to himself. He opens the chest. Inside are gold coins, about four thousand of them.

"Whoa. That's… a lot of gold. Gorgeous, but not exactly useful here." He takes twenty gold coins. About four thousand are left.

He hears Mara say, "James. I kept getting to where you'd just been. I'm Mara. I don't remember how I got here, just the boat, and this journal. There's a chest right beside us. Did you already look in the cabin?"

He starts to speak. While he speaks, he hears her again. "You're sitting. The chest is open, full of gold, and you've got some in your hand. I haven't touched it. Did you open it? What's in the cabin?"

His words come out loud: "This is a lot of gold, but not exactly useful right now. I took a few to examine. Want to check the cabin door with me? I'll head over there."

To himself he says, "Yeah, I opened it. It's full of gold. I haven't checked the cabin yet. Let's do it together. Ready?" He stands up and walks to the cabin door.

He hears Mara say, "Yes. Leave the rest of the gold. I'll come to the door with you."

He opens the cabin door and goes in. On a rough wooden table is an oil lantern.

To himself he says, "Lantern's half full. I'll check the fireplace for tinder or tools. Mara, can you see if there's a tinderbox tucked near the bed frame?" He inspects the stone fireplace from where he stands. The island tells him he would need to be closer for details.

He hears Mara say, "The lantern has oil, but it's bright enough in here without it. Bed's made. Fireplace is empty. I don't see anything that says who was here, or how we got here. Did you find anything outside besides the coins?"

He walks to the fireplace. Up close, the hearth is swept and empty. He inspects the narrow bed. It is neatly made, and empty.

He hears Mara say, "It's just a swept hearth. No ash, no wood. James. Do you remember anything from before you were here? A boat, a name, anything besides the journal?"

He answers only to himself. "I remember my name, James, and a porch with peeling paint, the smell of rain on hot asphalt. Then the hill here with the journal. No boat. Let's check that ladder and the platform above." Mara does not hear any of this.

He walks for about two minutes to the tall metal ladder.

"Mara, I'm at the tower. Going to climb up and take a look. Join me if you want." He says this to himself, and starts to climb.

On the rungs he says to himself, "Steady rhythm, hands, feet." He sees a person below, too far to make out clearly, walking. "Still climbing. I'll meet you at the top, Mara."

At fifteen minutes and twenty-one seconds he reaches the platform, a hundred feet up. On a table are a lighter, a sword and a snorkel.

He picks up the lighter and the snorkel. The sword is steel, in a leather scabbard with a shoulder strap.

"I'll brace myself by the hatch and help Mara onto the platform before anything else." He does it. In his own words: "Kneel by the ladder opening, take a firm grip on the railing with one hand, and offer my other forearm to steady the young woman as she steps off the ladder onto the platform."

He waits. To himself he says, "She made it. Good."

He says out loud, "Hey, Mara. Nice work. Up here there's a bell on the northeast headland and a stone circle near the cliffs. Also a sword on this table. Do you want to carry it, or should I? I grabbed a lighter and a snorkel. What do you want to check next? Ring the bell to see if anyone answers, or go look at the stone circle?"

To himself he says, "I'll take the sword. Let's head to the bell next." He picks up the sword and puts it on. He starts down the ladder.

Then he hears Mara say, "Leave the sword. I don't want to carry it. I came up for the view. If we go anywhere after this, it's the bell. I want a minute up here before I climb down."

He keeps descending. To himself he says, "Got it, enjoy the view. I'll head down and wait for you at the base, then we'll go ring the bell."

At twenty-two minutes he steps off the ladder onto the ground. He takes off the sword and puts it down on the grass by the base.

He says out loud, "Take your time, Mara. I'm right here at the base. When you're down, we'll head toward the bell northeast."

He waits more than two minutes.

"Last rung. I've got you." He does something. In his own words: "Stand close to the ladder base and offer my forearm to steady the young woman as she steps off, then gesture northeast toward the headland path to the bell."

The sword stays on the grass. He walks to the stone circle, a low ring of fitted stones with grass in the middle. Then he walks on to the bronze bell.

He says out loud, "Ringing the bell now!"

He pulls the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island. He waits twenty seconds and listens.

To himself he says, "No one answered. I'll ring it once more, then just listen with Mara."

He says out loud, "One more ring, then let's listen a moment."

He pulls the rope again. The bell rings out across the island.

"Let's just listen a little while and watch the sea." He waits. Then he says to himself, "No one answered. Let's just sit a minute and watch the water. If this all blinks out, I'm glad I met you, Mara."

He sits down by the bell. He waits forty-five seconds. "I breathe and watch the line where water meets sky."

That is where his timeline ends, at thirty minutes, sitting by the bell on the headland. The last words he said out loud were "One more ring, then let's listen a moment." He wrote nothing in his journal. He carries a lighter, a snorkel, and twenty gold coins.
