His Story

A young man wakes on open rocky ground. He has a journal and a pencil. No one has told him that anyone else is on the island.

He says to himself, "I'm on open rocky ground with ocean not far to the north. The steel tower with the ladder and a high platform catches my curiosity. I wonder what the view is like up there."

Almost two minutes in, he sees a person far off, too far away to make out clearly. They are walking. He says to himself, "I spot someone off to the southwest, but I'll keep going to the ladder for the view."

He reaches the ladder. "The ladder looks solid. I'd like to see the island from the platform, and maybe what's up there, so I'll climb."

The climb takes four minutes. Near the top he says, "I can see the table on the platform. Looks like a lighter, a sword with a strap, and a snorkel and mask."

A little after six minutes, he reaches the platform, a hundred feet up. He sees the person again, still too far to make out. "From up here I can see most of the island. I notice someone to the west, curious who they are."

He shouts. These are the first words he says out loud on the island: "Hello! Up here on the tower. Hello!"

He looks at the sword. It is steel, in a leather scabbard with a shoulder strap. Then he hears a woman shout: "Hello! I'm coming over. Is it safe to climb? Anything I should know up there?"

He shouts, "Hello! I'm on the tower platform. Who are you?"

Then he shouts down an answer. "It seems safe! The ladder and platform feel solid. There's a railing up here."

The figure comes closer. At about sixty feet he can make her out. She is a young woman, about twenty, and she is walking.

He shouts again: "You're close. I can see you! It's safe to climb. Best to keep your hands free: you could tie your lantern, cup, and anything else to the rope and I'll haul them up after you're on the platform."

As she starts up the ladder, he speaks out loud: "Hey there. Welcome. It's solid. Take your time." He tells her how to carry her journal, pencil and cup so both hands are free for the rungs. He ends, "I'm Alex. What's your name?"

He waits two minutes at the top. While he waits, he talks to her, but only to himself. "Looking good, nice and steady." Then he says out loud, "You're doing great, about two-thirds up. The platform's clear." He asks her again whether she wants a hand. He ends, "I'm Alex."

He steps back from the top of the ladder and waits again. Then he says out loud, "You're almost at the top, nice and steady." Once more he asks if she wants a hand.

Close to twelve minutes in, he says to himself, "She made it. Nice."

He starts to speak, and while he speaks he hears her voice. "Hi, I'm Maya. Thanks for the spot, Alex. This is a great view. Mind if I check the table? Also, I left a lantern and a rope at the base. We could haul them up after."

He finishes what he had started saying: "Hey. Welcome up. Nice climb. I'm Alex. Want to set your cup down on the table and catch your breath?"

Then he answers her out loud. "Nice to meet you, Maya. Yep. Check the table. After, I can pop down and tie on your lantern and rope so we can haul them up, unless you'd rather leave them below."

He starts another long reply. While he speaks, he hears Maya say, "Alex, if you don't mind, could you pop down, tie the rope to the lantern handle, something secure like a bowline, and bring the other end back up? I'll be ready to haul from here and mind the edge of the platform."

His reply comes out: "Maya, sounds good. We can haul your lantern and rope up. One of us can pop down, tie them on, and the other can pull from up here. I'm happy to go down if you want to stay with the railing."

Then he answers what she asked. "On it. I'll climb down, tie a bowline to the lantern handle, and bring the free end back up to you. Keep a good stance and mind the railing. One at a time on the ladder."

He starts down. The descent takes three and a half minutes. "I'll shout up when the rope's secured to the lantern."

At seventeen minutes he steps off onto the ground. He picks up the coil of rope.

He tries to tie it on. In his own words: "Tie a secure bowline in the rope around the lantern's handle, leaving a long free end to take back up. Coil the free end neatly for climbing." The island has no rule for this. The lantern does not change. The rope is not tied.

He says to himself, "The island won't let me tie the rope to the lantern. I'll carry the lantern up carefully first, then I can come back for the rope."

As he starts to speak, he perceives the young woman, about a foot away, do something. In her words: "Take the free end of the rope from the young man, put a single turn around the platform railing for friction, and haul the lantern up hand-over-hand, keeping my weight low and staying clear of the ladder opening."

He says out loud, "Maya, quick update: the island's rules won't let me tie the rope to the lantern. I'll carry the lantern up carefully now, then come back down for the rope."

He picks up the oil lantern. He puts the rope down on the grass. He says to himself, "I'll sling the lantern so I have both hands free, then climb up."

He perceives her again. In her words: "Step back a couple of feet from the ladder opening, keep one hand on the railing, and hold the other hand ready to receive the lantern once Alex reaches the platform."

He says to himself, "I'll set the lantern down so I can climb and regroup with Maya up top." He puts the lantern down on the grass. "Lantern's down here for now. I'll climb up empty-handed."

He starts to climb. Six seconds later he perceives her once more, now at no distance at all. In her words: "Take the oil lantern carefully from Alex with both hands and set it on the platform table, then step back from the ladder opening to give him room."

He says to himself, "Got it. Thanks for catching the lantern. I'll keep climbing; please stay clear of the opening."

The climb takes four minutes. Again and again he says to himself, "Continuing up. Almost there. Please keep clear of the opening."

At twenty-two and a half minutes, he reaches the platform for the second time. He says out loud, "Nice teamwork. Thanks for getting the lantern up. I don't think the island will let us tie the rope, so we can leave it below for now unless you want me to climb back down later to fetch it." He asks again about the lighter.

He hears no answer. He says out loud, "Maya, are you heading down for the rope? I'll stay clear of the opening. If you pass the free end up, I can haul from here. One at a time on the ladder. Take your time."

He hears nothing. He says to himself, "I'll give you space on the ladder, Maya, one at a time." He waits a minute.

He picks up the sword from the table and puts it on. He sits down and waits again.

Nearly twenty-seven minutes in, he sees a young woman, about twenty, walking, sixty feet away. "I can see you moving out there."

He shouts, "Maya! You good out there? Do you still want the rope or should we leave it for now?"

Then, "Maya! Is that you by the stone circle? You good? Do you want me to come down or bring anything, or should I stay up here for now?"

He hears her shout back: "All good, Alex! I'm by the stone circle with the rope and lantern. Let's leave the rope for now. I'm heading to the bell. Meet me there when you can!"

He says to himself, "Heard you. She's good and heading to the bell. I'll head down to meet her." He shouts, "Copy, Maya! I'll head down now and meet you at the bell. One at a time on the ladder."

He starts down the ladder a second time. Halfway down he hears her shout: "Alex! Heads up. I'm about to give the bell a very gentle test ring. Cover your ears if you're close!"

He hears a loud bell ring. "Heard the bell. Loud but fine from here. Continuing down, three points on."

At thirty-two minutes he steps off the ladder and walks toward her. On the way he says, "Looks like Maya's writing in her notebook."

As he gets close, he hears her: "Over here by the bell, Alex! Lantern's lit low. After this, do you want to check the chest and crate by the shore or head toward the bridge and cabin?"

He reaches her and says out loud, "Nice gentle ring. Hey, Maya. Let's check the chest and crate by the shore first, then head to the bridge and cabin. Want me to carry the lantern? And do you want to bring the rope along or leave it here for now?"

He hears no answer. He looks around for a way down. "I don't see an easy way straight down here. Looks cliffy."

She moves, and he follows. He walks to her five times, south and then southwest. Between the walks he says to himself, "Let's head SW toward the beach to reach the shore. Safer than the cliffs. I can take point or follow you. Your call." None of this is said out loud.

At thirty-seven minutes he walks to the wooden crate on the beach.

He says to himself, "Hey, Maya. Crate's already open." He inspects it. It is empty.

While he looks, he perceives the young woman right beside him. In her words: "Kneel and use my hands to sweep and feel through the sand around the crate and along its edges, checking within about a 2‑foot radius and probing a few inches down for anything buried, cords, or markings. Then feel underneath the overhang if there's any gap."

He says to himself, "Good idea checking the sand." He reaches for the lantern. Someone else is holding it.

Then the woman hands him the oil lantern. He is holding it now. "Thanks for the lantern. I've got it. Heading to the rowboat."

He walks to the rowboat. He sets the lit lantern inside the boat and inspects it. The hull is dry and sound. Inside there is an oar, and the lantern. "Boat's clear. Maya, let's check the driftwood table for that chest. I'll grab the lantern and head there. Come with me?" He says this to himself too.

He takes the lantern to the driftwood table. When he gets there, he sees the young woman walking, a hundred and forty-seven feet away.

On the table is a sheet of paper. "This sheet looks blank." He sets the lantern down and looks closer.

He tries something. In his own words: "Pick up the sheet of paper, tip it to catch raking light from the lit lantern, and gently rub the side of the pencil lead over the surface with minimal pressure to try to bring out any impressed writing, stopping immediately if the paper scuffs." The island has no rule for this. The sheet of paper does not change.

"Blank paper stays here. I'll grab the lantern and head toward the bridge to say hello to whoever's nearby."

He walks to the wooden bridge, lantern in hand. To himself he says, "Maya, I'm heading toward the cabin after the bridge; call out if you want to link up." He does not say it out loud. He hears nothing from her.

He walks on to the wooden chest by the cabin. "Chest's open and empty. I'm heading to the cabin door. Maya, I'm by the door if you want to join."

The door is open. He steps inside and sets the lit lantern on the cabin table. He looks at the stone fireplace up close. The hearth is swept and empty.

"Fireplace is clear."

He walks to the narrow bed. He arrives at forty-four minutes and forty-six seconds. Fourteen seconds later, at forty-five minutes, his time on the island ends. He is standing by the bed in the cabin. The last words he said out loud were to Maya by the bell, eleven minutes earlier. In all that time, he has written nothing in his journal.
