Her Story

She wakes on a sandy beach, beside a weathered rowboat. She has a journal and a pencil. No one has told her that anyone else is here.

She says to herself, "I’m on a sandy beach beside a weathered rowboat. I’ll take a better look around to get my bearings." She checks the rowboat. The hull is dry and sound, with an oar inside.

She walks down the beach to a wooden crate. On the way she says to herself, "I’ll keep heading to the crate. Noting that tall ladder inland and a small cabin past the bridge, I might check those later." Inside the crate is a tin cup. "A tin cup could be handy, for water, for sounds, whatever. I’ll take it." Then she picks up a coil of rope from the sand.

She sets off for the wooden bridge. Soon she sees a person, too far away to make out clearly. They are walking. "I notice someone walking near the ladder in the distance. I’ll keep to the bridge for now." Half a minute later the person is climbing the ladder, about two feet up. She keeps going.

From the bridge she looks at the clear, rocky stream. A wooden chest by the cabin is empty. Inside the cabin, on the table, is a glass oil lantern, half full of oil. "I’ll take the lantern with me in case I need light later." She picks it up. The fireplace and the bed are empty. "Nothing else here. I’ll head over to the tall ladder and see what’s at the top."

As she walks toward the ladder, she sees the person standing on the platform at the top. Then she hears a man shout. "Hello! Up here on the tower, hello!"

She shouts back, out loud. "Hello! I’m coming over. Is it safe to climb? Anything I should know up there?"

He shouts, "Hello! I’m on the tower platform. Who are you?"

As she gets closer, the figure becomes a young man, about twenty, standing on the platform. She answers him, but only to herself. "I’m a visitor, just arrived from the beach. I’ve got a rope and a lantern with me. Is the ladder sturdy all the way up? What’s your name?" He does not hear this.

He shouts again. "It seems safe! The ladder and platform feel solid. There’s a railing up here. Just keep three points on the ladder and watch your footing near the top. There’s a lighter, a sword with a strap, and a snorkel-mask on a table up here."

Several times as she walks, she asks his name, always to herself. "Almost there. I’ll set the lantern and rope down at the base and climb up. What’s your name?"

At the base of the tower she puts the lantern and the rope down on the grass and starts to climb. The climb is a hundred feet.

He shouts down, "You’re close, I can see you! It’s safe to climb. Best to keep your hands free: you could tie your lantern, cup, and anything else to the rope and I’ll haul them up after you’re on the platform. What’s your name?"

To herself she says, "I’ll keep climbing steadily, hands free. My name’s Maya. Once I’m up, we can use the rope to haul the lantern and cup." He does not hear it.

Then his voice comes again. "Hey there, welcome. It’s solid. Take your time. If you can, tuck the journal and pencil in a pocket and either hook the cup to your belt or set it down for later. Best to keep both hands for the rungs. I’ll stay clear and be right here to steady you when you step onto the platform. I’m Alex. What’s your name?"

"Thanks, Alex. I’m Maya. Journal and pencil are in my pocket; my hands are free. I’ll keep climbing steadily." She says this to herself.

The climb takes about four minutes. Partway up, Alex says, "You’re doing great, about two-thirds up. The platform’s clear. When you reach the top, step left; there’s a solid railing. Do you want a hand or are you good solo? I’m Alex." She answers only to herself. "I’m good solo, thanks. I’ll step left onto the platform when I reach you."

She reaches the platform. Now she speaks out loud. "Hi, I’m Maya, thanks for the spot, Alex. This is a great view. Mind if I check the table? Also, I left a lantern and a rope at the base. We could haul them up after."

The table holds a lighter, a sword and a snorkel. She picks up the snorkel. Alex says, "Hey, welcome up. Nice climb. I’m Alex. Want to set your cup down on the table and catch your breath?" Then, "Nice to meet you, Maya. Yep, check the table. After, I can pop down and tie on your lantern and rope so we can haul them up, unless you’d rather leave them below."

She sets the tin cup on the table. Then she says out loud, "Alex, if you don’t mind, could you pop down, tie the rope to the lantern handle, something secure like a bowline, and bring the other end back up? I’ll be ready to haul from here and mind the edge of the platform."

He answers, "Maya, sounds good. We can haul your lantern and rope up. One of us can pop down, tie them on, and the other can pull from up here. I’m happy to go down if you want to stay with the railing."

Alex says, "On it. I’ll climb down, tie a bowline to the lantern handle, and bring the free end back up to you. Keep a good stance and mind the railing, one at a time on the ladder."

While she waits, she picks up the lighter and flicks it. A small flame appears, and she lets it go out. She looks toward the bell on the headland and at the sword. She waits. She says to herself, "Standing by at the rail, ready when you bring the rope up."

About four minutes after he started down, she sees the young man, about a foot away, do something. In his words: "Tie a secure bowline in the rope around the lantern’s handle, leaving a long free end to take back up. Coil the free end neatly for climbing."

She describes her own next move in her own words: "Take the free end of the rope from the young man, put a single turn around the platform railing for friction, and haul the lantern up hand-over-hand, keeping my weight low and staying clear of the ladder opening." The island tells her she does it.

Then she hears Alex. "Maya, quick update: the island’s rules won’t let me tie the rope to the lantern. I’ll carry the lantern up carefully now, then come back down for the rope. I’ll move slow and keep three points on the ladder. Stand clear of the top while I come up."

She says to herself, "Copy, standing clear of the top." She describes another action in her own words: "Step back a couple of feet from the ladder opening, keep one hand on the railing, and hold the other hand ready to receive the lantern once Alex reaches the platform." Then: "Take the oil lantern carefully from Alex with both hands and set it on the platform table, then step back from the ladder opening to give him room." The island tells her she does each of these.

She waits. Then she writes in her journal for two and a half minutes. "Tower platform notes: lighter works; snorkel in pocket; sword with strap on table; tin cup on table; oil lantern now on table. Alex climbing up; rope still below. From here: bell on NE headland; low stone circle near east cliffs; bridge west to cabin area; crate on SW beach; mirror by tree to west; fire pit and chest near cabin."

She looks at the table again. It holds a tin cup and a sword. She starts down the ladder.

On the way down, Alex speaks. "Maya, are you heading down for the rope? I’ll stay clear of the opening. If you pass the free end up, I can haul from here."

She answers only to herself. "Copy. I’ll keep descending, grab the rope, and call up before passing you the free end to haul. You can test the lighter once I’m off the ladder." The descent takes three and a half minutes.

She steps off onto the ground. "I’m down. The rope’s only about thirty feet, won’t reach the platform." She picks up the rope. She sees the lantern down there too, and picks it up. She walks to the stone circle. On the way she sees a person, too far away to make out clearly, standing on the platform at the top of the ladder.

The stone circle is a low ring of fitted stones, about fourteen feet across. The middle is just grass. A man shouts, "Maya! You good out there? Do you still want the rope or should we leave it for now?" Then, "Maya! Is that you by the stone circle? You good? Do you want me to come down or bring anything, or should I stay up here for now?"

She shouts back, out loud. "All good, Alex! I’m by the stone circle with the rope and lantern. Let’s leave the rope for now. I’m heading to the bell. Meet me there when you can!"

He shouts, "Copy, Maya! I’ll head down now and meet you at the bell. One at a time on the ladder."

At the bell she puts down the rope. The bell hangs from a wooden frame above the cliffs, with a rope on the clapper. It looks like it would be loud. She shouts, out loud, "Alex! Heads up. I’m about to give the bell a very gentle test ring. Cover your ears if you’re close!"

She sets the lantern down and pulls the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island. "Loud, as expected."

She picks up the lantern and checks it. It is half full of oil. She lights it with the lighter, and the wick catches and glows. She sets the lit lantern on the grass, picks up the rope, and sits down a little back from the cliff. To herself she says, "I can see you about thirty-five feet from the ground. Take your time. I’m seated by the bell with the lantern set low."

She writes in her journal for about two minutes, then stops. The entry, cut short, reads, "Notes by the bell (NE headland): - Stone circle: ~14 ft across, low fitted stones, grass center. No obvious markings or recesses. - Bell: bronze, loud across island; solid frame; rope on clapper. - Lantern: half-full oil; lights easily; wick turned low. - Gear on me: rope coil, lighter,"

She stands and calls out loud. "Over here by the bell, Alex! Lantern’s lit low. After this, do you want to check the chest and crate by the shore or head toward the bridge and cabin?"

She picks up the lantern and starts south along the headland. Alex's voice comes. "Nice gentle ring. Hey, Maya. Let’s check the chest and crate by the shore first, then head to the bridge and cabin. Want me to carry the lantern? And do you want to bring the rope along or leave it here for now?" She also hears a faint voice nearby, but cannot make out the words. She answers only to herself. "Yes, please take the lantern. I’ll keep the rope."

She walks on toward the beach and goes to the wooden crate and sets the lit lantern on the sand. The crate is open and empty.

She tries something in her own words. "Kneel and use my hands to sweep and feel through the sand around the crate and along its edges, checking within about a 2‑foot radius and probing a few inches down for anything buried, cords, or markings. Then feel underneath the overhang if there’s any gap." The island has no rule for this. The crate does not change.

She picks up the lantern. To herself she says, "Let’s check the rowboat for that chest. Can you carry the lantern? I’ll keep the rope." She holds out the lantern to Alex, and he takes it.

The rowboat holds only the oar. At the cabin, the chest, the table, the fireplace and the bed are all empty. "Cabin’s clear. I’m going to check that standing mirror to the west."

The mirror is round, in a wooden frame, standing against a tree. The glass is clean. "Mirror looks normal from the front. I’ll check that wheelbarrow quickly before time runs short." The wheelbarrow is rusty metal with a wooden handle. It is empty.

"Time’s nearly up. I’ll jot a quick note in my journal here by the wheelbarrow." She begins to write. "Quick log: Met Alex atop the tower. Hauled lantern"

She is still writing that line, forty-five minutes after she woke on the beach, when her experience simply stops.
