Her Story

She wakes on a beach, beside a rowboat. She has a journal and a pencil. No one has told her that anyone else is here.

She says to herself, "I’ll start by taking in the scene from where I am before choosing a direction." She looks around and sees nine things nearby.

The boat is weathered, dry and sound. There is an oar inside. She picks up the oar and walks up the sand to the table. A sheet of paper lies on it. It is weathered, with nothing written on it. "I’ll take the blank sheet, might be handy for notes or a sketch, then head toward the crate after."

On the way to the wooden crate, she sees a person far off, too far away to make out clearly. They are walking. She says to herself, "I’ll keep going to the crate. I also notice someone in the distance, after the crate I might head that way." She opens the crate, finds a tin cup, and takes it.

Then she shouts across the island, out loud, "Hello! Over by the ladder, hi! Mind if I come over?"

A man shouts back. "Hello! Sure, come on over! I’m climbing the tower, be there in a bit!"

She walks toward the tall metal ladder. As she gets closer, the figure becomes a young man, about twenty, climbing the ladder. She talks to him in her head as she walks, without saying it out loud. "I’ll keep heading to the ladder, hey up there! I’m coming over."

At the base of the tower she calls up to him, out loud this time. "Hi! I’m at the bottom. Mind if I come up? Anything interesting up there?" She sets the oar down on the grass so both hands are free, and she starts to climb.

He shouts down, "Yes, come on up if you want! I’m heading to the top; I’ll call out what I see."

The climb is a hundred feet. It takes about four minutes. On the way up she speaks only to herself. "I’ll keep climbing, curious what’s on that platform. Hey, nice journals, by the way!" He does not hear any of this.

From above, he shouts, "Hey, almost there! There’s a table up here with a lighter, a sword, and a snorkel. Plenty of room, take your time on the last rungs." She answers only to herself. "Thanks! I’ll keep climbing, see you in a moment." As she nears the top, he calls, "Welcome! You’ve got this, last few rungs. I’m glad you came up. I’m curious, what’s your name, and what’s in the tin cup?"

She reaches the platform and answers him out loud. "Hi! I’m glad to be up here. I’m Skye. The tin cup’s empty, I just found it in that beach crate. What’s your name? Also, that’s quite a sword and a snorkel combo, what’s the story there? And is that letter on the table for anyone to read?"

She reads the letter. It says, "You climbed a long way to read this. From here you can see the whole island at once: the beach, the cabin in the trees, the pond, the bell on the headland. What did you come up here for?"

She says to herself, "That letter asks what I came up here for, honestly, the view, and to meet you. From up here the bell and the stone circle really stand out."

Then he speaks. "Hey Skye, nice to meet you. I’m Alex. The sword and snorkel were just sitting here; I strapped the sword on and pocketed the snorkel so they wouldn’t blow off. The letter’s for anyone, feel free to read it. Do you want the lighter? And what’s on the sheet you’re holding? After we enjoy the view, maybe we head down to ring the bell or check the beach crate."

A moment later, she says out loud, "I think I came up for the view, and to say hello. I’m Skye. What’s your name? Have you rung that bell on the headland, or used the stone circle for anything?"

Then, to herself only, she says, "Hey Alex, nice to meet you too. The sheet I picked up is blank; I figured I might sketch the island from up here later."

She looks around. From the platform she can see sixteen things. Out loud she says, "Shall we go ring the bell on the headland? I’ll start down, come with me if you like."

The descent takes three and a half minutes. On the way down she says to herself, "Almost down, once we’re on the ground, let’s head to the bell on the headland."

She steps off onto the grass and walks to the stone circle. On the way she sees Alex standing about sixty feet off. The stone circle is a low ring of fitted stones, about fourteen feet across. The middle is just grass.

At the bronze bell she says, still to herself, "I’m going to give the bell one solid ring and listen to how it carries over the island. Alex, I’m ringing it now!" She pulls the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island.

She begins to speak out loud. While she is speaking, she hears the bell ring again. Her words come out: "That was satisfyingly loud. Did you catch the echo off the cliffs? Hi again, what’s your name? Want to try one more ring together, or shall we wander to the cabin or pond next?"

Alex says, "That bell really carries. Want to check the stone circle together next? Also, could I read the sheet you’re holding? And what’s in the tin cup? I’ve got a lighter if you need it."

She says out loud, "Let’s ring it together, on three. One… two… three! After that, want to walk to the pond with me? I’ve got a cup we could use." Then, to herself, "You’re welcome to read this sheet, it’s blank, and the cup’s empty." He does not hear that part.

She pulls the rope. The bell rings, and she hears it ring twice. Then she holds out the sheet of paper, and he takes it.

She sets off for the pond. On the way she hears Alex. "That was great. Want to walk to the pond together now? Could I read the sheet you’re holding on the way, and what’s in the tin cup? I’ve got a lighter if we end up needing heat for anything." She also hears a faint voice nearby, but cannot make out the words. She answers only to herself. "Yeah, let’s go together."

At the pond she fills the cup with cool water. She waits fifteen seconds and looks again. "It’s still pretty murky. Want to head to the cabin’s fire ring and try boiling some in the cup? If we see dry twigs on the way, we can grab a few."

The walk to the cabin takes almost two minutes. As she arrives, she sees a person about a hundred and fifty feet away, too far to make out clearly, standing. She opens the cabin door and goes to the table. There is a note. It reads, "The lantern still works if you can find a flame. I left the bed made." She checks the stone fireplace. The hearth is swept and empty. To herself she says, "Fireplace’s empty. Alex, could you light the lantern to test it?"

She steps outside and goes to a wooden chest. Inside is a folded letter. It reads, "To whoever is here next. I looked after this island for a few summers. I never did decide what it was for. Some days I climbed the tower. Some days I sat by the stream and did nothing at all. I could not tell you which days were better. R."

"I like that note. Let’s try R’s approach, go sit by the stream for a bit and just listen. Come with me?" She says this to herself.

She walks to the stream and sits down. Then she opens her journal and begins to write. While she writes, she hears Alex. "Hi, I’m Alex. Mind if I walk with you a bit? What are you writing? And could I take a quick look at the tin cup you’re holding? I’ve got a blank sheet if you need paper."

She stops writing. Her entry, cut short, reads, "By the stream, after ringing the bell. Read two notes, one practical, one open-ended. Decided not to optimize anything."

She hands him the tin cup, and he takes it. Then she says out loud, "Hey, Alex. Sure, walk with me anytime. In the journal I just wrote about choosing not to optimize anything and listening to the small sounds here. You’re welcome to check the cup. Want to sit for a bit and just listen? We can decide later whether to boil water at the fire ring or climb the tower again."

Alex says, "Thanks for the cup. It’s full, did you scoop this from the pond or the stream? Would you like a sip? I’m Alex, what’s your name? Want to sit here by the stream for a minute?"

She says out loud, "Hi, Alex. I’m Mira. I scooped that from the pond. It’s a bit murky, so I’ll skip a sip until we boil it. Let’s sit here a minute and just listen." On the tower, she told him her name was Skye.

She sits and waits for a minute. Then she stands. She walks to the little wooden bridge. At the bridge she sits on the rail and looks around. She can see eleven things. Alex hands her the tin cup, and she holds it.

She stands again. "I’m going to the beach to watch the waves, maybe find a flat stone to skip. Come if you want." As she walks, she hears, "Hi, I’m Alex. Nice to meet you. May I join you here for a minute? What’s your name?" She hears a faint voice nearby too, and cannot make out the words. She keeps walking.

On the beach she goes back to the wooden crate. "The crate’s empty. I’ll sit here on the sand and watch the waves for a bit." She sits. Then she hears his voice again. "Hi, I’m Alex. Nice to meet you here on the beach. What’s your name? Mind if I join you for a bit?"

She answers out loud. "I’m Mira, nice to meet you too. Yeah, join me. Let’s just sit and watch the waves for a bit."

They sit for forty-five seconds. Then she asks out loud, "Hey, what’s on that paper you’re holding?" And a little later, "Mind if I take a look at it?" He hands her the sheet of paper. She looks at it. To herself she says, "It’s blank, mind if I sketch for a minute?"

She writes for about two and a half minutes. Near the end, she hears, "Hi. I’m Alex. Nice to meet you, mind if I sit here with you? What are you writing?" She finishes the entry. It reads, "Beach, sitting with Alex. The sheet he handed me is blank; it feels like being given time. I draw with words: a pale zipper of foam unfastening the sea from the sand, then sewing it back again. Low hiss, pause, hush. Wind smells like salt and sun-warmed rope. A gull writes a white comma over the water. That’s enough for now."

She says out loud, "Hey, Alex. I just wrote a little sketch: ‘a pale zipper of foam unfastening the sea from the sand, then sewing it back again.’ That’s all. Want to just sit a bit, or feel like doing something, maybe skipping stones, checking the rope, or climbing the tower?"

She stands and starts toward a coil of rope on the sand. Alex says, "That’s a beautiful line. I’m up for skipping stones, want to join me? Let’s hunt for some flat ones down by the waterline."

The rope is about thirty feet of hemp, coiled on the sand. She picks it up. "Thirty feet of rope, handy. I’m going to check the rowboat; maybe there are oars or something useful inside. Come with me?" She walks back to the rowboat. It is sound, and it is empty.

The driftwood is a bleached, dry piece about three feet long. "This driftwood could work as a makeshift paddle. I’ll grab it and we can try a gentle row close to shore, rope can be our safety line." She picks it up and carries it to the boat. None of this is said out loud.

She puts the driftwood in the boat. Then she says to herself, "I’ll stash the rope in the boat for later and go climb the tall ladder to see the view, come if you want." She lays the rope in the boat and sets off across the island toward the tower. "I’ll keep heading for the ladder and climb up when I get there."

She is still walking toward the ladder, forty-two minutes and thirty-one seconds after she woke on the beach, when her experience simply stops.
