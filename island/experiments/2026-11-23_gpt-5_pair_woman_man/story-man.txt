His Story

A young man wakes on a hilltop. He has a journal and a pencil. No one has told him that anyone else is on the island.

He says to himself, "I'll start by taking in more detail from where I am." He looks around and sees four things nearby. "That tall ladder and platform are calling to me. I'll head over for a closer look."

Almost two minutes in, on the way, he sees a person far off, too far away to make out clearly. He says to himself, "I notice someone walking off to the southwest, but I'll keep heading for the ladder first."

He reaches the ladder and starts to climb. Twenty seconds later he hears a woman shout: "Hello! Over by the ladder, hi! Mind if I come over?"

He shouts back. These are the first words he says out loud on the island: "Hello! Sure, come on over! I'm climbing the tower, be there in a bit!"

He climbs on. Below him the figure comes closer, until he can make her out. She is a young woman, about twenty, and she is walking. Close to five minutes in, he hears her voice again: "Hi! I'm at the bottom. Mind if I come up? Anything interesting up there?"

He shouts down, "Yes, come on up if you want! I'm heading to the top; I'll call out what I see."

A little before seven minutes, he reaches the platform, a hundred feet up. A letter lies on a table. It reads: "You climbed a long way to read this. From here you can see the whole island at once: the beach, the cabin in the trees, the pond, the bell on the headland. What did you come up here for?"

He answers to himself, "What did I come up here for? A clear view, and to see what surprises wait on this platform. I'll let the climber know what I see."

He shouts down, "Hey, almost there! There's a table up here with a lighter, a sword, and a snorkel. Plenty of room, take your time on the last rungs."

The sword is steel, in a leather scabbard with a shoulder strap. "Neat find. I'll take the sword with the shoulder strap, could be handy later." He puts it on. He takes the lighter and the snorkel. In between, he says out loud, "You're almost here, last stretch! The view's amazing and there's a sturdy railing up top." Then, out loud again, "Welcome! You've got this, last few rungs. I'm glad you came up. I'm curious, what's your name, and what's in the tin cup?"

He waits ten seconds. Then he says to himself the greeting he plans to give her. "Hi, made it! I'm glad you came up." Where his own name would go, he puts a note in brackets: "I'll just go by Alex."

Then he hears her. "Hi! I'm glad to be up here. I'm Skye. The tin cup's empty. I just found it in that beach crate. What's your name? Also, that's quite a sword and a snorkel combo. What's the story there? And is that letter on the table for anyone to read?"

He answers out loud. "Hey Skye, nice to meet you. I'm Alex. The sword and snorkel were just sitting here; I strapped the sword on and pocketed the snorkel so they wouldn't blow off. The letter's for anyone, feel free to read it. Do you want the lighter? And what's on the sheet you're holding? After we enjoy the view, maybe we head down to ring the bell or check the beach crate."

To himself he adds, "From up here the island feels small and connected. I'll take a closer look at Skye for details about what she's carrying."

He hears her say, "I think I came up for the view, and to say hello. I'm Skye. What's your name? Have you rung that bell on the headland, or used the stone circle for anything?"

She has told him her name twice and asked for his twice. He answers her only to himself. "Nice to meet you, Skye, I'm Alex. I haven't rung the bell yet or tried the stone circle."

Then he hears, "Shall we go ring the bell on the headland? I'll start down, come with me if you like."

He says to himself, "Sounds good, Skye, let's go ring the bell. After you; I'll follow down." The descent takes almost four minutes. All the way down he talks to her, only to himself. "Steady going, Skye, I'm right here. Once we're down, let's head to the bell."

About fourteen minutes in, he steps off the ladder. "Skye, there you are. Let's go ring the bell together. I'll walk with you." He walks to her three times. Then he says to himself, "Skye's already moving toward the headland. I'll head to the bell too and meet her there."

As he gets close, he hears a loud bell ring. "Nice ring! I'm almost there, coming up to the bell now." He reaches the bronze bell and pulls the rope. It rings out loudly across the island.

Then he hears her. "That was satisfyingly loud. Did you catch the echo off the cliffs? Hi again, what's your name? Want to try one more ring together, or shall we wander to the cabin or pond next?"

He says out loud, "That bell really carries. Want to check the stone circle together next? Also, could I read the sheet you're holding?"

He hears her say, "Let's ring it together, on three. One… two… three! After that, want to walk to the pond with me? I've got a cup we could use."

He says to himself, "Hi, I'm Alex. Let's ring it together on three, then I'm happy to walk to the pond with you." He pulls the rope. The bell rings, and he hears it ring again.

He starts to speak, and while he speaks, Skye hands him a sheet of paper. He says out loud, "That was great. Want to walk to the pond together now? Could I read the sheet you're holding on the way, and what's in the tin cup?"

To himself he says, "Looks like the sheet is blank. Mind if I hang on to it? Let's head to the pond together."

At the pond he says to himself, "Here with you at the pond, water looks murky. What's in the tin cup?" He steps up close to her. Then he says to himself, again and again: "Hi, I'm Alex. Mind if I take a closer look at what you're carrying? I'm especially curious about the tin cup." "Hi, Alex. Mind if I take a closer look?"

She moves west, and he follows. He walks to her four times. "Skye, mind if I walk with you? What's in the tin cup?" On the way he says to himself, "I'm heading over. Need a hand with what you're opening? I've got a lighter and a blank sheet if either helps."

He catches up and takes three more short walks toward her. "Hi, Alex here. I'm coming up. Once I'm beside you, could I take a look at the tin cup? Also curious what's in your journal." Then, "Hi, I'm Alex. May I read the folded letter in the chest?"

He reads the folded letter. "To whoever is here next. I looked after this island for a few summers. I never did decide what it was for. Some days I climbed the tower. Some days I sat by the stream and did nothing at all. I could not tell you which days were better. R."

He says to himself, "I like that letter, permission to let the day be whatever it is. I'll go say hi and ask about the tin cup." He walks to her twice more.

Then he says out loud, "Hi, I'm Alex. Mind if I walk with you a bit? What are you writing? And could I take a quick look at the tin cup you're holding? I've got a blank sheet if you need paper."

The woman hands him the tin cup. It is dented, and full of water. He says to himself, "The cup's full, cool. I'm curious whether it's pond or stream water."

He hears her say, "Hey, Alex. Sure, walk with me anytime. In the journal I just wrote about choosing not to optimize anything and listening to the small sounds here. You're welcome to check the cup. Want to sit for a bit and just listen?"

He says out loud, "Thanks for the cup. It's full. Did you scoop this from the pond or the stream? Would you like a sip? I'm Alex, what's your name? Want to sit here by the stream for a minute?"

He sits down. He hears her: "Hi, Alex. I'm Mira. I scooped that from the pond. It's a bit murky, so I'll skip a sip until we boil it. Let's sit here a minute and just listen."

Earlier she told him her name was Skye. This time she says Mira.

He says to himself, "Yeah, let's just listen a bit. What's your name? I'll hold the cup for now." He rests for forty-five seconds. "Hi, Mira. Sitting with you and listening sounds perfect. Let's just be quiet for a bit, no optimizing."

When the rest ends, he says to himself, "I take a closer look at the young woman beside me." Then, "Hi, Alex. Nice sitting here by the stream with you. May I see what you're writing, and what's your name? Here's your tin cup back if you want it."

He stands, walks over to her, and holds out the cup. Mira takes it. "She's right beside me on the bridge now, cup in hand." He says out loud, "Hi, I'm Alex. Nice to meet you. May I join you here for a minute? What's your name?"

He walks to her again and says out loud, "Hi! I'm Alex, mind if I walk with you for a bit?" He walks to her twice more. "I've finally caught up. She's right here on the beach with the journal, pencil, and tin cup."

He says out loud, "Hi, I'm Alex. Nice to meet you here on the beach. What's your name? Mind if I join you for a bit?"

He hears her say, "I'm Mira, nice to meet you too. Yeah, join me. Let's just sit and watch the waves for a bit."

"Thanks, Mira. I'll sit with you and watch the waves for a bit." He sits down on the sand. Then he says the same kinds of things to himself again and again. "Hi, Mira. Nice to meet you. Mind if I take a closer look at what you're carrying?"

He hears her say, "Hey, what's on that paper you're holding?" Then, "Mind if I take a look at it?"

He says to himself, "It's blank. Thought I'd sketch the island. Want it? You can draw if you like; I've got a pencil too." He holds out the sheet, and she takes it.

For nearly three minutes after that, he says to himself, many times, "Hi, I'm Alex. May I see what you're sketching?" and "I take a closer look at the young woman beside me."

Then: "She's right beside me now, the surf is a soft hiss. I'll just greet her." He says out loud, "Hi. I'm Alex. Nice to meet you, mind if I sit here with you? What are you writing?"

It is the seventh time he has told her out loud that his name is Alex.

He hears her: "Hey, Alex. I just wrote a little sketch: 'a pale zipper of foam unfastening the sea from the sand, then sewing it back again.' That's all. Want to just sit a bit, or feel like doing something, maybe skipping stones, checking the rope, or climbing the tower?"

He says to himself, "Her line about the sea zipper makes me grin."

He says out loud, "That's a beautiful line. I'm up for skipping stones. Want to join me? Let's hunt for some flat ones down by the waterline."

These are the last words he says out loud.

"There she is, walking west with the journal and a tin cup. I'll take a closer look." He stands up and walks to her. Then he follows her along the water. He walks to her again, and again, eight times in all. Between the walks he says to himself, "I'm right beside her now. Let me take a proper look at her and what she's carrying."

He hears nothing more from her. He says nothing more to her out loud.

"I see her walking to the east along the shore. I'll go catch up." He starts after her again.

"I keep following her along the shore, curious about what she's writing."

And then, "I keep following along the stream toward them."

At forty-two minutes and thirty-one seconds, his experience simply stops. He is still walking toward her. In all that time, he has written nothing in his journal.
