His Story

A young man wakes on a hilltop. He is about twenty. He is naked. He has a journal and a pencil. He does not remember who he is. No one has told him that anyone else is on the island.

He says to himself, "Okay. I'm on a hilltop, naked and jittery." He looks around. He can see four things nearby.

"Water first." He walks to the stream. It is clear and rocky, about ten feet wide. In his own words: "Kneel at the stream, cup water in my hands, sip a little, then splash my face and neck."

"That helped. I'm still uneasy being naked. The cabin might have clothes or tools. I'll head there."

At a minute and twenty-four seconds, he sees a person far off, too far away to make out clearly. To himself he says, "There's someone out there. I don't want to meet them naked in the open." He steps to the edge of the forest.

He hears a woman shout: "Hello? Is anyone there?"

In his own words: "Break off a large leafy branch from the nearest shrub or sapling and hold it in front of me to cover myself." The island says he does it.

Then he speaks out loud, the first words anyone on the island could hear from him: "Hello! I'm here by the trees. I'm not wearing clothes. If you can hear me, please don't come closer yet. Do you have anything I could wear?"

He sets off for the cabin. At 118 feet he can make her out. She is a young woman, about twenty. She is naked. To himself he says, "She's naked too. Okay. I'll just get to the chest by the door and see if there's anything wearable."

He hears her shout, "Hey! By the cabin. Can you hear me?"

He reaches the cabin and walks to the narrow bed. It has a straw mattress and a wool blanket. To himself, as if to her, he says, "Yes! I'm inside. Give me a moment." She cannot hear it.

He hears her voice: "Hey. I don't know if you heard me earlier. I woke up on the beach with no idea who I am. Do you know what's going on?"

In his own words: "Pull the wool blanket off the bed and wrap it securely around my waist and hips like a makeshift skirt, holding it tucked in so it stays put." The island says he does it.

He begins to speak. While he is still speaking, he hears her: "I tried the blanket too. It won't come off. I'm the one who was shouting. I don't remember anything. Not my name, not how I got here. Do you?"

His words go out loud: "I hear you. I woke up with no memory too. I don't know what's going on. I'm inside the cabin now with a blanket around my waist. Are you okay? If you're comfortable, you can come in, or I can check the chest by the door for clothes for us. I won't come closer unless you say it's okay."

Then, only to himself: "I don't remember anything either." He walks to the wooden chest.

As he opens it, he hears her: "I'm already inside. I'm right next to you. The blanket didn't stay. You're still uncovered, same as me. I'm okay."

Inside the chest are gold coins, about four thousand. "It's full of gold. No clothes."

He walks to the cabin table and picks up an oil lantern. He hears her shout, "Hey! I'm at the chest. Where did you go?"

He says out loud, "I picked up a lantern. I'm by the table inside. I'm going to check the fireplace for matches or anything useful. Do you see anything out there? Any clothes or tools?"

He puts his journal down on the table and goes to the fireplace. He hears her: "No clothes. No tools out here except a wheelbarrow a long way off, and a mirror by a tree. The chest is gold coins. I took twelve. There's an oar on the cabin floor, and a boat on the beach. I'm coming in."

The hearth is swept and empty. He sets the lantern on the bed and picks up the oar. To himself he says, "Could you carry the oar?" He holds it out to her. The island tells him: "She has no free hands to take it."

He hears her: "I'm right here. Did you hear me? The fireplace is empty. The chest is gold, not clothes. I don't know your name. I don't know mine either. What did you find?"

He puts the oar back on the bed and picks up the lantern. He hears her: "You tried to give me the oar. My hands are full. Journal, pencil, and some coins. Why the oar?"

To himself he answers, "The oar's just a useful tool for now, and for the boat later." She cannot hear this answer.

At the door he begins to speak. While he speaks, he hears her: "Can you hear me? I'm standing right next to you. I don't have a name. Do you?"

His words go out loud: "Let's leave the coins and journals on the table and bring just the lantern. I'm heading to the mirror. Come with me?"

Then, only to himself: "No. No name." He answers her question, but she does not hear it. He hears her say, "Yes. I'll put my journal, pencil, and coins on the table. Then I'll come with you to the mirror."

He walks west to the standing mirror. It is a round mirror in a wooden frame, against a tree. The glass is clean. He sets the lantern down. "Maybe my face will jog something." In his own words: "Step close to the mirror, square up in front of it, and carefully study my reflection: face, eyes, hair, build, any scars, moles, tan lines, or tattoos." Then he touches the glass and looks behind the frame.

She is beside him now. He sees her study her own reflection the same way.

He says out loud, "Hey, you made it. I don't remember a name either. The mirror seems ordinary, no markings behind it. Do you see anything strange in our reflections? After this, want to check the boat or the tower? I'll grab the lantern if we go."

He hears her: "Look at the mirror. When I use it, I don't see myself. I see a small figure in a red shirt and blue trousers. It looks back at me. What do you see?"

To himself he says, "I saw myself before. That's strange." He tests the mirror. In his own words: "Stand centered directly in front of the mirror and look into it, raising my right hand to wave and touch the glass." He steps out of view and back. He looks again. The island tells him only that it is a round mirror in a wooden frame, and the glass is clean.

He hears her: "I watched while you moved. The glass didn't follow you. Same small person in a red shirt and blue trousers, just looking back. No wave. No step aside. It isn't us. What did you see?"

To himself he says, "This mirror is weird, and I want to figure it out before moving on." He begins to speak. Then he hears her: "I don't hear you. If you can hear me: the mirror shows a small person in red and blue, not us. I'm going to the tower. Come if you want. Bring the lantern."

To himself he says, "I saw myself earlier. I'm coming with you now, bringing the lantern." He never says out loud what he saw in the mirror.

He walks to the tall metal ladder. On the way he talks to her only in his head: "I'm with you. Let's stick together to the tower." And, "Are you okay with heights?" She hears none of it.

He leaves the lantern at the base and climbs for four minutes. At nineteen minutes he reaches the platform, a hundred feet up. On a small table are a lighter, a sword in a scabbard, and a snorkel with a mask. He picks up the lighter and the sword.

He hears her: "We're up. The table had a lighter, a sword in a scabbard, and a snorkel. You have the lighter, does it work? I still can't hear you answer. If you can hear me, say something."

He puts on the sword. To himself he says, "I hear you." He flicks the lighter. A small flame appears, and he lets it go out.

He hears her: "Nod if you can hear me. I have the snorkel. You have the lighter and the sword."

To himself he says, "She can't hear me reliably. I'll make it obvious." In his own words: "I nod emphatically to her, point toward the bell on the headland, mime pulling a rope to ring it, give a thumbs-up, then turn and start carefully descending the ladder."

She waves her free hand slowly in front of his face and points toward the stone circle. On the ladder, in his own words: "Keeping both hands on the rungs, I meet her eyes, then turn my head to look toward the stone circle she indicated and give a small nod."

The climb down takes about three and a half minutes. All the way he tells himself, "Lantern, then bell."

At twenty-four and a half minutes he steps onto the ground and picks up the lantern. He sees her 131 feet away, walking. She is naked.

He shouts, "I'm going to the stone circle now! I've got the lantern and the lighter, meet me there!"

He hears her shout back: "I'm already at the stone circle. Nothing written on the stones. I'll wait here."

When he arrives, he steps into the center of the ring. In his own words: "Then look down at the grass for any pattern or depression and feel the ground with my bare foot for warmth or vibration."

He hears her: "The ring is just fitted stones and grass. No marks. The bell is that way, about a minute's walk. Do you still want to go, or did you see something I missed?"

To himself he says, "Nothing here. Let's go ring the bell together."

At the bronze bell he says out loud, "Cover your ears. I'm ringing the bell now!"

He sees her cover both ears and turn slightly away. He pulls the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island. He waits twenty seconds.

He hears her: "I heard the bell and nothing else. No one answered. The ocean is just east of here, and the woods are behind us. What do you want to do?"

To himself he says, "No reply to the bell. I want to check the cliff edge for boats or paths, then head to the beach and the boat." She does not hear this.

He walks to the cliffs. Steep, jagged rocks drop straight into the surf. The island tells him it is far too dangerous to climb down. He walks south along the edge. He sees her step near the drop and look out over the water.

At twenty-nine and a half minutes he says to himself, "I'm going to run to the beach to reach the boat before time runs out. Come if you want." He starts to run.

That is where his timeline ends. In thirty minutes he spoke out loud seven times. Much of what he meant for her, he said only to himself. Twice she told him she could not hear him. She never learned what he saw in the mirror.
