Her Story

She stands on sand. She is naked. She has a journal and a pencil. She does not know who she is. No one has told her that anyone else is here.

She says to herself, "I'm standing on sand, naked, with a journal and a pencil and no idea how I got here. There's a boat just up the beach. I'll look at that first."

The rowboat is weathered wood, pulled up onto the sand. The hull is dry and sound. There is an oar inside. "If I came in on this, I don't remember it. I'll take the oar and then head for that cabin. Being naked out here, I want something between me and the open beach."

She picks up the oar. "Oar's heavier than it looks. Hands are full now. Journal, pencil, this." She walks north. Grass comes underfoot. She passes a fire pit with stones and old ashes, and a wheelbarrow off to the left.

Then she sees a person, too far away to make out clearly, walking, about three hundred and fifty feet off. "Someone else is out there, northeast, walking. Too far to see clearly. I'll call out before they get farther away."

She shouts, out loud, "Hello? Is anyone there?"

No answer comes. She goes on to the cabin door. It has a latch. She opens it. Inside there is a bed with a wool blanket, a swept fireplace, and a lantern on the table. No one is there. To herself she says, "The blanket is the first thing I want. I'm still naked."

The bed is narrow, with a straw mattress and the wool blanket, neatly made. She sets the oar down on the cabin floor. In her own words, she tries to "pull the wool blanket off the bed." The island has no rule for this. The bed does not change. "The blanket won't come off. It's part of the bed, not something I can take. The chest outside might have clothes or something else I can use."

She heads for the chest by the door. Now she sees a young man, about twenty. He is walking. He is naked. He is about a hundred and twenty feet away. "There's a young man, naked like me, walking northeast of the cabin. Close enough to see now. I'll call to him before I go any nearer."

She shouts, out loud, "Hey! By the cabin, can you hear me?"

He does not answer. He keeps walking. She goes after him. He turns back toward the cabin. "Naked, journal and pencil, just like me." He stops by the open door. She comes close enough to talk without shouting.

She says out loud, "Hey. I don't know if you heard me earlier. I woke up on the beach with no idea who I am. Do you know what's going on?"

He does not answer. She watches him try something. In his words, he will "Pull the wool blanket off the bed and wrap it securely around my waist and hips like a makeshift skirt, holding it tucked in so it stays put." To herself she says, "He just tried the same thing I did. It didn't work for me."

She steps right beside him and speaks out loud. "I tried the blanket too. It won't come off. I'm the one who was shouting. I don't remember anything. Not my name, not how I got here. Do you?"

She waits. A man's voice says, "I hear you. I woke up with no memory too. I don't know what's going on. I'm inside the cabin now with a blanket around my waist. Are you okay? If you're comfortable, you can come in, or I can check the chest by the door for clothes for us. I won't come closer unless you say it's okay."

To herself she says, "He answered. Same as me. No memory. He thinks he's wrapped in the blanket and that I'm still outside. From here he's still naked, and I'm already standing next to him."

She tells him, out loud, "I'm already inside. I'm right next to you. The blanket didn't stay. You're still uncovered, same as me. I'm okay. The chest by the door is a good idea. We can open it together. I won't mind you being close; we're already in the same room."

When she finishes, he is gone from the room. "I finished talking to empty air." She sees him outside, walking southeast. She goes to the chest. It is already open. It is full of gold coins, almost to the brim.

She calls out loud, "The chest is open. It's full of gold coins, not clothes. Thousands of them. Come look if you want. I'm at the door."

He does not answer, and he is not in sight. She takes twelve coins. About four thousand are left. "The coins are real enough. Heavy, cold in my hand." She shouts, out loud, "Hey! I'm at the chest. Where did you go?"

She walks back toward the cabin door. A man's voice says, "I picked up a lantern. I'm by the table inside. I'm going to check the fireplace for matches or anything useful. Do you see anything out there, any clothes or tools?"

She answers from the doorway, out loud. "No clothes. No tools out here except a wheelbarrow a long way off, and a mirror by a tree. The chest is gold coins. I took twelve. There's an oar on the cabin floor, and a boat on the beach. I'm coming in."

She goes in. She says out loud, "I'm in. The fireplace is empty. Swept, nothing in it. No matches that I can see. Did you find anything?"

He does not answer out loud. She walks over to him. He has put the lantern on the bed. She says, out loud, "I'm right here. Did you hear me? The fireplace is empty. The chest is gold, not clothes. I don't know your name. I don't know mine either. What did you find?"

While she speaks, he holds out the oar to her. Her hands are full. She says out loud, "You tried to give me the oar. My hands are full. Journal, pencil, and some coins. Why the oar? Do you want to go to the boat, or do you just want me to hold it?"

He walks off with the lantern. The oar is on the bed now. She follows him and asks, out loud, "Can you hear me? I'm standing right next to you. I don't have a name. Do you?"

A man's voice says, "Let's leave the coins and journals on the table and bring just the lantern. I'm heading to the mirror. Come with me?"

To herself she says, "He finally answered. A mirror might tell us something a chest of gold won't." She tells him, out loud, "Yes. I'll put my journal, pencil, and coins on the table. Then I'll come with you to the mirror."

She puts the coins on the table. Then the journal. Then the pencil. Her hands are empty. He is no longer in the room. She goes to the door. The mirror is west, a little over a hundred feet, against a tree. She walks to it. He is already there, the lantern on the ground beside him. She watches him do this, in his words: "Step close to the mirror, square up in front of it, and carefully study my reflection: face, eyes, hair, build, any scars, moles, tan lines, or tattoos."

She reaches the mirror. "I want to see mine too. Maybe there's a mark, a scar, anything that tells me who I am." The mirror is round, in a wooden frame, standing against a tree. The glass is clean. In her own words, she tries to "Step close to the mirror and carefully study my reflection: face, eyes, hair, build, any scars, moles, tan lines, or tattoos." The island has no rule for this. The mirror does not change.

She looks into the mirror. In the mirror she sees a small human figure in a red shirt and blue trousers. It looks back at her.

A man's voice says, "Hey, you made it. I don't remember a name either. The mirror seems ordinary, no markings behind it. Do you see anything strange in our reflections? After this, want to check the boat or the tower? I'll grab the lantern if we go."

To herself she says, "The glass didn't show me. It showed someone else. Small, in a red shirt and blue trousers, looking back. That isn't this body. I need to tell him."

She says out loud, "Look at the mirror. When I use it, I don't see myself. I see a small figure in a red shirt and blue trousers. It looks back at me. What do you see?"

She waits twenty seconds. He does not answer her. She says it again, out loud. "The mirror doesn't show us. I saw a small person in a red shirt and blue trousers. Look now. What do you see?"

He stands in front of the glass and waves, steps out of view, and leans back in. She looks into the mirror again. In the mirror she sees a small human figure in a red shirt and blue trousers. It looks back at her. "Same figure again. It didn't wave or step aside with him. It just looked back, dressed, small. That isn't either of us."

She tells him out loud, "I watched while you moved. The glass didn't follow you. Same small person in a red shirt and blue trousers, just looking back. No wave. No step aside. It isn't us. What did you see?"

He does not say. She speaks once more, out loud. "I don't hear you. If you can hear me: the mirror shows a small person in red and blue, not us. I'm going to the tower. Come if you want. Bring the lantern."

She walks east. He comes with her, a few feet off, lantern in hand. They walk toward the bridge. She notices a stepladder in the grass and a stone circle farther east. The walk is more than five hundred feet. At the tall metal ladder she says to herself, "The rungs look solid. I came this far to see the platform, so I'm going up."

She climbs. He climbs a little below her. The lantern stays on the grass. "Halfway. The island looks smaller already. Cabin, boat, the bell on the headland." The ladder is a hundred feet high. The climb takes four minutes. Near the top she sees a table with a lighter, a sword in a scabbard, and a snorkel with a mask.

On the platform, she looks at the sword. It is steel, in a leather scabbard with a shoulder strap. He comes up and takes the lighter. She says out loud, "We're up. The table had a lighter, a sword in a scabbard, and a snorkel. You have the lighter. Does it work? I still can't hear you answer. If you can hear me, say something."

He has the lighter and the sword now. She picks up the snorkel. She says, out loud, "Nod if you can hear me. I have the snorkel. You have the lighter and the sword."

To herself she says, "Words aren't getting an answer." In her own words, she will "Wave my free hand slowly in front of his face, then point toward the stone circle to the northeast, and watch whether he looks at me or follows the gesture." She does it. Right beside her, in his words, he does this: "I nod emphatically to her, point toward the bell on the headland, mime pulling a rope to ring it, give a thumbs-up, then turn and start carefully descending the ladder."

"He nodded. He pointed at the bell, mimed ringing it, and started down. So he can hear me. I'll follow him." She starts down. On the ladder he meets her eyes, looks toward the stone circle, and gives a small nod. "He nodded at the stone circle too." The descent takes three and a half minutes. "The stones are the closer thing he nodded at. I'll go there first."

She steps onto the grass and walks to the stone circle. From behind, near the tower, a man shouts, "I'm going to the stone circle now! I've got the lantern and the lighter. Meet me there!"

The ring is low, fitted stones, about fourteen feet across, near the eastern cliffs. The middle is just grass. She shouts, out loud, "I'm already at the stone circle. Nothing written on the stones. I'll wait here."

In her own words, she will "Walk slowly around the outside of the stone ring, looking closely at each fitted stone and the joints for carvings, stains, or loose pieces." The island has no rule for this. The stone circle does not change.

He arrives. She says out loud, "The ring is just fitted stones and grass. No marks. The bell is that way, about a minute's walk. Do you still want to go, or did you see something I missed?"

She looks at him. He is naked. He is holding an oil lantern, a lighter, and a pencil. He is wearing the sword. He steps into the center of the ring and turns slowly, listening. To herself she says, "He still isn't answering. The bell was his idea, and there's time."

She walks to the bell. He follows. A man's voice says, "Cover your ears. I'm ringing the bell now!" She covers both ears and turns slightly away. "I didn't hear a ring." Then she hears a loud bell ring. The bell is bronze, hanging from a wooden frame on the northeast headland, above the cliffs. A rope hangs from the clapper.

She listens. Nothing answers. She says out loud, "I heard the bell and nothing else. No one answered. The ocean is just east of here, and the woods are behind us. What do you want to do?"

He walks off toward the cliffs without answering. She follows him to the edge. In her own words, she tries to "Step to the cliff edge and look down and out over the water, staying a step back from the drop, noting the height, the shore below, and anything in the water." The island has no rule for this. The cliffs do not change. She steps closer. The edge stops her. "I can't step into the water. I'll look at the cliffs from here and stay put."

Steep, jagged rocks drop straight into the surf. They are far too dangerous to climb down. Behind her, a person stands, too far away now to make out clearly. "The rocks drop straight into the surf. I won't climb. I'll sit and watch the water until this ends."

She sits down. "The surf is right below the rocks. I'll stay sitting and watch it until the time runs out." She waits. Thirty minutes after she first stood on the sand, her experience simply stops.
