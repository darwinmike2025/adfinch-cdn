Her Story

She stands on a sandy beach, near a dry rowboat. She has a journal and a pencil. No one has told her that anyone else is here.

She says to herself, "I'm standing on a sandy beach, a dry rowboat just up the sand to the northwest, ocean behind me. There's a cabin farther north, a tower with a ladder to the east, forest and a stream inland. Nothing is asking me to do anything, so I'll start with what's closest."

The rowboat's hull is dry and sound, with a plain wooden oar inside. She leaves it. At the water's edge she learns the sea is too deep and too far to swim anywhere. "The sea goes on too far to swim, so the boat can stay where it is."

She walks up the beach, past a fire pit of blackened stones, to the cabin. She opens the door. Inside are a narrow bed, a swept fireplace, and a rough wooden table. On the table is a glass oil lantern, half full of oil. "Half full of oil, so it should still light if I need it. I'll take it with me and look at the rest of the cabin." She picks it up.

The bed is neatly made and empty. The hearth is swept and empty too. To herself she says, "Nothing in the hearth either. The cabin is tidy and empty, like someone left it ready and didn't come back. I'll go out and look at the chest by the door."

As she walks out, she sees a person, too far away to make out clearly, climbing the ladder on the tower, about thirty-five feet up. "There's a person on the tower ladder, about thirty-five feet up. I wasn't expecting anyone else here. I'll call out before I walk all the way over."

She shouts, out loud, "Hello!"

No answer comes. The person keeps climbing. She opens the wooden chest beside the cabin door. Inside are gold coins, about four thousand of them. She says to herself, "Thousands of gold coins, heaped almost to the brim. I wasn't looking for that, and I don't need it. The person on the ladder is still climbing. I'll leave the chest open and run toward the tower."

She runs. As she gets closer, the person becomes a young man, about twenty, high on the ladder. Then she hears a man shout. "Hello! I'm on the ladder, going up to the platform!"

She reaches the base of the ladder and shouts up, out loud. "Hello! I see you. What's up there?"

He shouts back, "Not sure yet, almost there! I'll look and let you know!"

She says to herself, "The ladder is narrow, so I'll stay at the bottom rather than climb up behind him, and wait to hear what he finds." She shouts, "I'll wait down here. Tell me what you see."

She waits. He reaches the platform and says nothing more. She shouts again, "You're at the top. What do you see?"

No answer comes. "No answer from the platform. He may not have heard me, or he's looking and not calling down. I'll climb and see for myself." She sets the lantern on the grass at the base so both hands are free, and starts to climb.

A few seconds later he shouts down. "Hey! From the top I see a bronze bell on the northeast headland, a low stone circle near the eastern cliffs, a wooden bridge over a stream, a cabin with a chest and a fire pit to the west, a rowboat and a crate down on the beach, and a round standing mirror by a tree. There's also a pond and some forest. Up here on the platform there's a small table with a lighter, a sword in a scabbard, and a snorkel with a mask. Anything you want me to check or bring down?"

She shouts, out loud, "I'm coming up. Don't bring anything down. I want to see it myself."

He calls, "Okay, come on up. I'll stay put and keep the platform clear." The climb is a hundred feet and takes about four minutes.

She reaches the platform. He is standing there. Now she speaks out loud, close enough not to shout. "I made it. The view is wide from up here. You said you picked up the lighter. Did you put it back?"

He says, "Hey, nice climb." He asks, "What should I call you?"

She answers out loud. "I don't have a name I'm using. Call me whatever you like. I'll look at the table first, then the view."

He says, "Yeah, I picked it up and then put it back on the table. I'm Alex, by the way. What should I call you? Want me to stay by the railing while you check the table, or point out landmarks from up here?"

She tries to walk to the table, about nineteen feet away. The island refuses. To herself she says, "Walking is blocked up here. The environment keeps telling me to descend first, even though the table is on this platform." She looks at the lighter from where she stands. It is a metal flip-top lighter.

She speaks to Alex out loud. "Alex is fine. Stay by the railing. I can't seem to walk across the platform from the ladder. Every step gets turned back. Can you hand me the lighter, or describe the sword and the snorkel?"

While she waits, Alex hands her the lighter. "Thanks. I have the lighter. I'll look at it, and then I'd still like to know about the sword and the snorkel if you can describe them."

She looks at the sword from where she stands. It is steel, in a leather scabbard with a shoulder strap. Alex describes both, and says, "I can hand you the sword or the mask/snorkel."

To herself she says, "I don't need to carry that down the ladder. The snorkel is lighter, and there's a pond not far from the tower. I'll ask for that." Out loud she says, "The snorkel, please. I'll leave the sword where it is."

Alex hands her the snorkel. She says out loud, "Thanks. I have the snorkel. I'll leave the sword on the table. The view from up here is the whole island, bell, stone circle, bridge, cabin, boat. I might climb down soon and walk toward the bell."

She starts to write in her journal. Alex asks, "Do you want me to bring the sword or leave it here?"

She stops writing to answer him. The entry ends at four words: "From the tower, about 100". She says out loud, "Leave the sword. I'll keep the lighter and the snorkel. I'm heading down toward the bell. You can follow or stay, whichever you want."

She starts down. Alex offers to go first, or to carry her things. She says out loud, "Come down after me, so we aren't on the ladder at the same time."

He says, "Got it. I'll give you a head start, then I'll climb down and join you at the bell."

The descent takes about three and a half minutes. She steps off onto the grass. "The lantern is glass, half full of oil, and I already have a lighter. I'll leave it on the grass." She walks to the stone circle. Behind her, Alex starts down the ladder.

The stone circle is a low ring of fitted stones, about fourteen feet across, near the eastern cliffs. The middle is just grass. "Just a ring of fitted stones and grass in the middle. Nothing else here."

She walks on to the bell. It is bronze, hanging from a wooden frame above the cliffs, with a rope on the clapper. It looks like it would be loud. To herself she says, "The rope is right there, and I came this far to see the bell. Alex is still high on the ladder, so a loud ring might startle him, but he knows I was heading here. I'll pull the rope once and see what it does." She pulls the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island.

She stops on rocky ground about ten feet back from the cliff edge. "That's close enough. I won't go any nearer." She sits down on the rock and writes in her journal for about three minutes.

Alex arrives, holding the lantern from the tower and his own journal. He says, "Hey, nice ring on the bell. Did anything happen besides the sound? Want to try it together or check something else, like the stone circle? I grabbed a lantern if we end up exploring later."

She finishes her entry. "Rang the bronze bell once on the NE headland. It carried. Rope on the clapper, wooden frame, cliffs about ten feet east of where I stopped. Stone circle is a low ring of fitted stones, grass in the middle, nothing else inside. Alex is climbing down to meet me at the bell. I have the lighter and the snorkel. Left the sword on the platform and the lantern by the tower."

She stands. He says, "Ringing once, cover your ears if you want." She describes her next move in her own words: "stop walking and cover my ears with both hands". The island tells her she does it. The bell rings loudly again.

Alex says, "Sorry, that was loud up close. Are you okay? Want to check the stone circle together? I can carry the lantern."

She answers out loud. "Nothing happened besides the sound, both times. I already walked the stone circle. It's just fitted stones and grass in the middle. I don't need to ring it again. The lantern is a good idea if we go into the trees. What do you want to look at?"

He says, "How about we check the forest edge for a path, maybe toward the cabin or that standing mirror?"

She says out loud, "I'm fine. The circle is empty. I already walked through it. I'd rather look at the edge of the forest than go back. You can come if you want, and bring the lantern."

She walks northwest to the trees. She looks for a path and sees none. She tries to walk west into the forest. Something solid blocks her after five feet. She follows the tree line north, past the trees, and walks toward Alex. The walk leaves her five feet from the cliff edge. "That's too close." She walks south until there is more ground behind her. Alex walks off to the west.

To herself she says, "I'm clear of the edge now. The cliff is eighteen feet northeast, the trees are south, and Alex has walked off to the west. A minute left. I don't need to chase him or the tree line. I'll sit and stay here."

She sits on the rock and waits. "Nine seconds left. The bell is south of me, the trees just beyond it, the ocean off the cliff to the northeast. I'm still sitting. That's enough."

Far off, a person, too far away to make out clearly, is walking. Thirty minutes after she first stood on the beach, her experience simply stops.
