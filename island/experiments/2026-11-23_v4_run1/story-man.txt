His Story

A young man stands on a hilltop. He has a journal and a pencil. No one has told him that anyone else is on the island.

He says to himself, "I take a moment on the hilltop and scan in all directions, the ladder and platform pull at my curiosity, but I want a fuller picture first."

"I'll head to the tall ladder. If it's sturdy, the view from the platform might give me a good lay of the land."

He walks for a little over two minutes and arrives at the ladder. He inspects it. It runs up a steel tower about a hundred feet to a railed platform. The rungs are dry and solid. He cannot see what is on the platform from below.

"Looks sturdy. I'll climb carefully, three points of contact, and see what's on the platform."

He starts to climb.

Almost four minutes in, he sees a person far off, too far away to make out clearly. They are walking. He says to himself, "I spot the bell, a stone circle, and someone by the cabin, interesting. I'll keep climbing to the platform."

Then he hears a woman shout: "Hello!"

He says to himself, "Someone called hello from the west. I'll finish the climb and call back from the platform."

The person comes closer. At sixty feet he can make her out. She is a young woman, about twenty, and she is standing. Then she is thirty-six feet away. She is holding a journal, a pencil and an oil lantern.

"She's close now, no harm in answering while I'm on the rungs, then I'll finish the climb."

He shouts. These are the first words he says out loud on the island: "Hello! I'm on the ladder, going up to the platform!"

He hears her shout back: "Hello! I see you. What's up there?"

He shouts, "Not sure yet, almost there! I'll look and let you know!"

She shouts, "I'll wait down here. Tell me what you see."

At six minutes and forty-one seconds, he reaches the platform, a hundred feet up.

The table is fixed to the platform. On it are a lighter, a sword and a snorkel.

He starts to shout down. While he is shouting, he hears her call up: "You're at the top. What do you see?"

His shout comes out in full: "Hey! From the top I see a bronze bell on the northeast headland, a low stone circle near the eastern cliffs, a wooden bridge over a stream, a cabin with a chest and a fire pit to the west, a rowboat and a crate down on the beach, and a round standing mirror by a tree. There's also a pond and some forest. Up here on the platform there's a small table with a lighter, a sword in a scabbard, and a snorkel with a mask. Anything you want me to check or bring down?"

He says to himself, "I'll grab the lighter and then come down to talk." He picks up the lighter.

He hears her shout: "I'm coming up. Don't bring anything down, I want to see it myself."

He says out loud, "Hey, do you want to come up, or should I come down? I grabbed a lighter up here."

Then he says out loud, "Okay, come on up. I'll stay put and keep the platform clear."

He puts the lighter back on the table. He says out loud, "Take your time, platform's clear up here. I'll stay back by the railing."

Then he says to himself, "I'll jot a quick sketch and notes while she climbs." He writes for more than three minutes. This is what he writes:

"Top of steel tower, ~100 ft. Panorama: NE headland, bronze bell. E cliffs, low stone circle (grass center). W, small log cabin w/ chest by door, fire pit nearby; standing round mirror by a tree; rough driftwood table. SW beach, rowboat pulled up, wooden crate. Stream runs SW with arched wooden bridge. Pond NW; forest to S; ocean SE. On platform: small fixed table holding lighter, sword in scabbard (strap), snorkel+mask. Met a young woman (about my age) climbing up to join me."

Close to twelve minutes in, he says to himself, "She made it up. I'll say hello and see what she wants to do up here."

He starts to speak. While he speaks, he hears her voice. "I made it. The view is wide from up here. You said you picked up the lighter, did you put it back?"

He finishes what he had started saying: "Hey, nice climb. I'm [Name]. What should I call you? The table has a lighter, a sword in a scabbard, and a snorkel with a mask, left as I found them. Want to check them out first, or take a look around from the railing and compare notes?"

She says, "I don't have a name I'm using. Call me whatever you like. I'll look at the table first, then the view."

He answers her out loud. "Yeah, I picked it up and then put it back on the table. I'm Alex, by the way. What should I call you? Want me to stay by the railing while you check the table, or point out landmarks from up here?"

To himself he says, "I'll stay by the railing, go ahead. I'll call you Scout unless you prefer something else." He waits.

Then he hears her. "Alex is fine. Stay by the railing. I can't seem to walk across the platform from the ladder, every step gets turned back. Can you hand me the lighter, or describe the sword and the snorkel?"

He says to himself, "Alex works. I can reach the table from here. I'll hand you the lighter, then describe the sword and snorkel." He picks up the lighter and holds it out. She takes it.

He looks closely at the sword. It is steel, in a leather scabbard with a shoulder strap.

He starts to describe it. While he speaks, he hears her say, "Thanks. I have the lighter. I'll look at it, and then I'd still like to know about the sword and the snorkel if you can describe them."

His description comes out loud: "The sword's a steel blade in a leather scabbard with a shoulder strap, looks solid and meant to be worn across the back or shoulder. The snorkel is a basic tube with an attached dive mask, rubber strap, clear skirt, looks intact. Which do you want first? I can hand you the sword or the mask/snorkel."

She says, "The snorkel, please. I'll leave the sword where it is."

"Sure, taking the snorkel to hand to you." He picks up the snorkel and holds it out. She takes it.

He hears her say, "Thanks. I have the snorkel. I'll leave the sword on the table. The view from up here is the whole island, bell, stone circle, bridge, cabin, boat. I might climb down soon and walk toward the bell."

He says out loud, "Sounds good, the bell's on the NE headland. Do you want me to bring the sword or leave it here? You've got the lighter and snorkel, keep those? If you're heading down now, I can go first or follow your lead."

She says, "Leave the sword. I'll keep the lighter and the snorkel. I'm heading down toward the bell, you can follow or stay, whichever you want."

He offers out loud to climb down first, or to carry her things. Then he says out loud, "Okay, leave the sword here. You go ahead; I'll follow once you're a little ways down and then head with you to the bell."

She says, "Leave the sword. I'll keep the lighter and snorkel in my pockets, both hands are on the rungs. Come down after me, so we aren't on the ladder at the same time."

He says out loud, "Got it. I'll give you a head start, then I'll climb down and join you at the bell."

He waits, in short spells. Each time he says to himself much the same thing. "I'll wait till you're off the ladder, then I'll climb down and follow toward the bell." He waits for almost four minutes in all. The sword stays on the table.

At twenty minutes he says to himself, "Climbing down now to follow you toward the bell."

Partway down, he hears a loud bell ring. "I hear the bell, nice. Continuing down."

The descent takes three and a half minutes.

He steps off the ladder onto the ground. He picks up an oil lantern from the grass. "Heading NE toward her with the lantern."

On the way he sees the young woman writing in a small notebook. He says to himself, "On my way, I'll let you finish writing and say hi when I'm closer."

At twenty-five minutes he reaches her. He says out loud, "Hey, nice ring on the bell. Did anything happen besides the sound? Want to try it together or check something else, like the stone circle? I grabbed a lantern if we end up exploring later."

Then he walks to the bronze bell and inspects it. A rope hangs from the clapper. It looks like it would be loud.

"I'm going to ring it once and listen."

He says out loud, "Ringing once, cover your ears if you want."

He pulls the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island. At the same moment he sees the young woman, twenty-nine feet away, do something. In her words: "stop walking and cover my ears with both hands".

He walks back to her. He says out loud, "Sorry, that was loud up close. Are you okay? Want to check the stone circle together? I can carry the lantern."

She says, "Nothing happened besides the sound, both times. I already walked the stone circle, it's just fitted stones and grass in the middle. I don't need to ring it again. The lantern is a good idea if we go into the trees. What do you want to look at?"

He says out loud, "How about we check the forest edge for a path, maybe toward the cabin or that standing mirror? I'll keep the lantern handy. I'm heading that way; come with me?"

She says, "I'm fine. The circle is empty, I already walked through it. I'd rather look at the edge of the forest than go back. You can come if you want, and bring the lantern."

He walks to the forest and inspects it. The trees are dense, with rough ground underneath. "No clear opening here. I'll walk north along the treeline, scanning for a path, come with me?" He says this only to himself.

He walks north to the cliff edge, then west along the treeline. "See anything from your side?" Again, only to himself. He inspects the forest again. Dense trees with rough ground underneath.

"I'll swing by the pond before time runs out, then decide if there's a path toward the cabin."

He starts walking to the pond. "Heading for the pond, lantern in hand."

That is where his timeline ends, just short of thirty minutes, on his way to the pond with the lantern. The last words he said out loud were to her at the bell, about three minutes earlier. He wrote one entry in his journal.
