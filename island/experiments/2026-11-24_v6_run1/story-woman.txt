Her Story

She stands on warm sand beside a rowboat, with the water close behind her. She has a journal and a pencil. No one has told her that anyone else is here.

She says to herself, "Sand under my feet, dry and warm. A rowboat is pulled up just ahead of me, and the water is close behind. I don't remember how I got here. I'll look at the boat first."

The rowboat is weathered wood, dry and sound, with one oar inside. She walks to the water's edge. The sea surrounds the island. It is too deep and too far to swim anywhere. "Too far to swim, and I don't want to. The cabin is the only thing that looks like someone might have lived here. I'm going to walk to it."

She walks inland onto grass, past a fire pit. "Grass now, and a fire pit ahead of the cabin. Someone built things here. I'll keep going." The walk is about three hundred feet. At the cabin she notices a chest by the door, and a mirror against a tree off to the left. The door is latched. She opens it. "Empty, from what I can see. A bed made up, a cold hearth, a lantern on the table. Nobody home. I'm going in."

The lantern is glass, half full of oil. The narrow bed has a straw mattress and a wool blanket, neatly made. It is empty. To herself she says, "Nothing under the blanket, nothing in the hearth. If anyone left a trace, it's more likely in that chest by the door than in here."

She starts toward the chest. Then she sees a person, too far away to make out clearly, climbing the ladder on a tower, about thirty-eight feet up. "There's someone on the ladder, halfway up the tower. I wasn't alone. I don't want to startle them from the ground, so I'll call out before I go any closer."

She shouts, out loud, "Hello! Down here, by the cabin!"

No answer comes. The person keeps climbing. She leaves the chest and walks toward the tower. She passes the stream and walks along the edge of the forest. The figure climbs higher. At about a hundred and fifty feet, she can see it is a young man, about twenty. To herself she says, "A young man, about my age, still climbing and still silent. There's a stepladder on the grass nearby, which is odd next to a hundred-foot tower. I'll get to the base first."

She reaches the base of the ladder about two minutes after leaving the cabin. He is nearly at the top. She speaks out loud, in a normal voice. "Hey. I'm down here. Can you hear me?"

Nothing comes back right away. "Still nothing. He's nearly at the platform, and whatever is up there is why he climbed. I'm going up after him." She starts to climb.

A man's voice calls down. "Yeah, I hear you! I'm just going up for a quick look. Back in a minute!"

She keeps climbing. The voice comes again. "Hey! The view's great up here. There's a lighter, a sword, and a snorkel on a little table. Want me to leave them put, or bring something down for you?"

To herself she says, "A lighter, a sword, and a snorkel. Strange set of things." She shouts up, out loud, "Leave them! I'm coming up."

He calls, "You're almost here, want to come up? I'm Marco. Want me to pass anything down, lighter, snorkel, or the sword, or are you coming to take a look?"

To herself she says, "Marco. He has a name, and he's waiting at the top instead of coming down. I already told him I'm coming up. I'll answer him properly when I get there."

The ladder is a hundred feet high. The climb takes about four minutes. Partway up she says to herself, "Halfway isn't even close yet. The ground is far enough that I don't want to look down. I'll just keep my hands on the rungs." Later, "Three quarters of the way. The ocean is on one side, the cabin a small thing on the other. I'll finish the climb." Near the top she can see the table. "Almost at the railing. I can see the table now. Lighter, sword, snorkel, just as he said. A few more rungs."

She reaches the platform. To herself she says, "The whole island is laid out below us. Cabin, beach, pond, that stone circle, the bell on the headland. And he's right here, waiting."

She speaks to Marco out loud. "I'm Mara. I came up to look. Leave the things where they are for now. How long have you been here?"

He does not answer. She waits fifteen seconds. She looks at him. He is standing right beside her, holding a journal and a pencil. Then he speaks. "Hey, Mara. I'm Marco. I've been here maybe ten, eleven minutes. Saw you on the way up. How about you, just arrived? Want to catch our breath and take in the view? After that I was thinking of checking out that bell on the headland. And do those things on the table mean anything to you, the lighter, the sword, the snorkel?"

She tries to walk across the platform to the table. The island does not let her. To herself she says, "I can't walk across the platform. The ladder wants me to go down first. The table is still marked as within reach, so I'll look at it from here." The table is small and wooden, fixed to the platform. On it are the lighter, the sword, and the snorkel.

"None of those things mean anything to me. A lighter, a sword, and a snorkel on a table a hundred feet up. They look placed, not left. I'll tell him that, and that I only just got here."

She starts to answer, and Marco talks over her. "Nice to meet you, Mara. Yeah, only about ten minutes for me. How long for you? Anything up here you were specifically looking for, or just the view?"

She finishes, out loud. "Just arrived. Maybe half an hour, if that. I woke up by a boat with a journal and a pencil, same as you seem to have. The lighter, the sword, the snorkel. They don't mean anything to me. They look like someone put them there. The view is worth the climb, though. I can see the bell from here."

Then she adds, out loud, "Just the view. I wasn't looking for anything in particular. Leave the things. A minute to catch our breath sounds good, then the bell. I thought I heard one earlier, from near the cabin."

She looks around. Marco says, "Alright, let's head to the bell. I'll go first down the ladder and wait for you at the bottom." She stands and waits forty-five seconds. To herself she says, "He's already started down. I'll give him a head start so we're not on the same rungs, then follow."

She starts down. The descent takes about three and a half minutes. "The rungs are cold under my hands. He's below me, still climbing. I'll keep my eyes on the ladder and not the drop." From the bottom, Marco calls up. "I'll wait here for you. I think the bell's toward those cliffs to the southeast. Once you're down, let's head that way along the edge."

She steps off onto the grass. She says out loud, "I'm down. Southeast along the edge sounds right. I'll walk with you."

She starts southeast. Marco is walking another way. To herself she says, "He's not coming southeast. He's walking northeast, toward the stone circle and the headland where I actually saw the bell. I'll turn and go with him." She follows. He walks past the stone circle without stopping. She stops at it. It is a low ring of fitted stones, about fourteen feet across, near the eastern cliffs. The middle is just grass. "Just stones and grass. Nothing written, nothing in the middle."

Marco shouts, "I'm at the bell! Want me to ring it?"

She shouts back, out loud, "Wait! I'm almost there. Don't ring it till I can see it."

He waits. She reaches the bell. It is bronze, hanging from a wooden frame on the northeast headland, above the cliffs. A rope hangs from the clapper. It looks like it would be loud. To herself she says, "It's just a bell and a rope. Loud, if the look of it is honest. I came up here to hear it. I'll tell him I'm going to pull it once."

Marco says, "Hey, perfect timing. Want to do the honors, or shall we pull it together on three?"

She says out loud, "I wanted to see it first. It's a bell and a rope, nothing else. I'm going to pull it once. If you don't want to be next to it when it rings, step back."

A loud bell rings. She has not touched the rope. She says out loud, "You count. I'll pull on three." Then, to herself, "Too late for a count. It already rang, loud, right beside me. I want to know if he pulled it." Out loud she asks, "It already rang. Did you pull it?"

To herself she says, "It was loud, right in my chest. I didn't pull the rope." She waits. Marco says, "Not me. I was stepping back. Maybe the rope slipped. If you still want to pull, I'll count you in on three. Or we can just listen a bit."

She says out loud, "I'll pull it once. Count me in."

She waits eight seconds. Marco says, "One... two... three!" She pulls the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island.

She says out loud, "That was it. Louder than what I thought I heard by the cabin. I'm going to listen a minute and see if anything answers."

She stands and listens for a full minute. To herself she says, "A full minute and nothing answered. He's already walking south, toward the stones. I'll write the bell down before I follow, while it's still in my ears."

She writes for almost two minutes. While she writes, she sees Marco at the stone ring. He walks slowly around it, running his fingertips along each fitted block and peering into the joints. Then he steps into the grassy center, stands still, and listens. After that he writes too. Her entry reads, "The bell on the northeast headland rang once before I touched it. Marco said he didn't pull the rope. I pulled it once myself. Loud, close, nothing like the faint sound I thought I heard by the cabin. I listened a minute. Nothing answered."

She walks down to the stone circle. She looks at it again. It is the same low ring, with grass in the middle. She speaks out loud. "I'm here. Did you find anything on the stones?"

He does not look up. She waits. Then he says, "Hey, just a low ring of fitted stones, grass in the middle. No markings or seams that I could feel. Want to walk the forest edge with me and have a look?"

She says out loud, "No markings, then. I'll put my hand on one, and then I'll walk the forest edge with you."

He walks toward the trees. She describes her next move in her own words: "I crouch and put my palm flat on the nearest fitted stone, feeling for carvings, seams, or any warmth that isn't just sun." The island has no rule for this. The stone circle does not change. Marco calls, "I'm at the forest edge, going to take a quick look just inside. Want to come?"

To herself she says, "The stone didn't tell me anything the island would count. He already said the same. I told him I'd come to the trees."

She walks to the tree line. Marco is about sixty feet to the west. She tries to walk north into the trees. Something solid is in the way. She moves three feet and stops. She tries to walk to the forest itself, and it moves her nowhere. "Something solid is in the trees. I can't step in."

She shouts, out loud, "The trees won't let me in. Something solid. I'm coming along the edge."

She walks west after him. He shouts, "I'm at the pond! Water's murky. I'm going to check the shoreline for tracks or a gap into the trees, meet me here?"

She reaches the pond. It is still, and the water is murky near the banks. Marco crouches at the edge, scanning the mud. He says, "What are you looking at? I didn't find any clear tracks, just murky edges. See something I missed?"

She says out loud, "I'm here. The trees stopped me. Something solid, not just brush. Did you find tracks, or a way in?" Then, "Nothing you missed. Murky water, no tracks from where I stood. The forest was solid when I tried to step in. Not brush, something that stopped me. I don't see a way through from here."

Marco walks off toward the bridge. To herself she says, "He's walking off toward the bridge. I have less than a minute. I'll put down what I actually found."

She starts to write. The entry stops after five words: "Mara. Woke by a boat. Climbed". To herself she says, "That note is too long. There's half a minute left and it won't finish. I'll write less, so something actually gets down."

She begins again. "This one is short enough. I'll let it finish." The second try reads, "Bell twice. Stones blank. Forest solid. Pond murky. Marco". It stops there. Thirty minutes after she first stood on the sand, her experience simply stops.
