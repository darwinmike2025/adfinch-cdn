His Story

A young man stands on a hilltop. No one has told him that anyone else is on the island.

He says to himself, "I start by taking in the view from here on the hilltop." He looks around. He can see four things nearby.

"That ladder and platform are calling to me. I want the higher view."

He walks for about two minutes. He arrives at the tall metal ladder.

"Time to go up and see what's on that platform." He starts to climb.

Almost four minutes in, he sees a person far off, too far away to make out clearly. They are walking. He says to himself, "I spot someone by the cabin, interesting. I'll keep climbing and get the full view from the top."

Then he hears a woman shout: "Hello! Down here, by the cabin!"

To himself he says, "Someone called from by the cabin, sounds like a woman. I'll keep climbing and check in from the top."

The person keeps coming closer. At sixty feet he can make her out. She is a young woman, about twenty, and she is walking.

He hears her voice. "Hey. I'm down here. Can you hear me?"

At six minutes and nineteen seconds, he reaches the platform, a hundred feet up.

He says out loud, the first words he speaks on the island: "Yeah, I hear you! I'm just going up for a quick look, back in a minute!"

He says out loud, "Hey! The view's great up here. There's a lighter, a sword, and a snorkel on a little table. Want me to leave them put, or bring something down for you?"

He hears her shout: "Leave them! I'm coming up."

He says out loud, "You're almost here, want to come up? I'm Marco. Want me to pass anything down, lighter, snorkel, or the sword, or are you coming to take a look?"

To himself he says, "Alright, leaving everything where it is. I'll give you room to come up." He waits.

"While she climbs, I'll jot a quick note about the view and the plan to check the bell after." He writes for about two minutes.

Then he hears her voice. "I'm Mara. I came up to look. Leave the things where they are for now. How long have you been here?"

"I close the journal mid-sentence and turn to her." This is what he has written, and it stops partway through:

"Top of tower (~100 ft). Wind cool, steady. Steel platform hums faintly. On the table: lighter, sword (in scabbard), snorkel+mask. Young woman climbing up from the west, said to leave items. Island notes from here: bell on NE headland, stone circle near eastern cliffs, cabin/bridge/stream to W, boat+beach SW, pond/forest S/NW. After she"

He says out loud, "Hey, Mara. I'm Marco. I've been here maybe ten, eleven minutes. Saw you on the way up. How about you, just arrived? Want to catch our breath and take in the view? After that I was thinking of checking out that bell on the headland. And do those things on the table mean anything to you, the lighter, the sword, the snorkel?"

He looks at her. She is a young woman, about twenty, standing on the platform. She is holding a journal and a pencil.

He says out loud, "Nice to meet you, Mara. Yeah, only about ten minutes for me. How long for you? Anything up here you were specifically looking for, or just the view? I'm happy to leave the stuff where it is. Want to catch our breath for a minute, then head toward that bell?"

Mara says, "Just arrived. Maybe half an hour, if that. I woke up by a boat with a journal and a pencil, same as you seem to have. The lighter, the sword, the snorkel. They don't mean anything to me. They look like someone put them there. The view is worth the climb, though. I can see the bell from here."

He sits down for a moment, then stands up again.

Mara says, "Just the view. I wasn't looking for anything in particular. Leave the things. A minute to catch our breath sounds good, then the bell. I thought I heard one earlier, from near the cabin."

To himself he says, "If she heard a bell earlier, I'm even more curious what it does."

He says out loud, "Alright, let's head to the bell. I'll go first down the ladder and wait for you at the bottom."

He climbs down. It takes three and a half minutes.

At sixteen minutes he steps off the ladder onto the ground.

He says out loud, "I'll wait here for you. I think the bell's toward those cliffs to the southeast. Once you're down, let's head that way along the edge."

He inspects the cliffs. They drop straight into the surf.

He hears Mara say, "I'm down. Southeast along the edge sounds right. I'll walk with you."

To himself he says, "She's down. Let's go NE toward that stone ring by the cliffs, then angle toward the headland and the bell, keeping a safe buffer from the edge." He walks to the stone circle.

He inspects the circle. It is a low ring of fitted stones, about fourteen feet across. The middle is just grass. "Low ring, just grass inside. I'm going to the bell, meet me there?"

He walks to the bronze bell. "Made it to the bell. I'll check in with Mara before I ring it."

He shouts, "I'm at the bell! Want me to ring it?"

He waits eight seconds. Then he hears Mara shout: "Wait! I'm almost there. Don't ring it till I can see it."

He says to himself, "I'm going to ring it once, Mara!" He starts to ring the bell. Then he says to himself, "Got it! Stopping. I'll wait till you can see it. We can ring it together if you like." The bell does not ring. He waits.

He says out loud, "Hey, perfect timing. Want to do the honors, or shall we pull it together on three?"

Mara says, "I wanted to see it first. It's a bell and a rope, nothing else. I'm going to pull it once. If you don't want to be next to it when it rings, step back."

At twenty minutes and five seconds he says to himself, "Let's pull it together. One... two... three." He starts to ring the bell.

He hears a loud bell ring. The bell rings across the island. The island tells him, "You pull the rope. The bell rings out loudly across the island."

He says to himself, "Your pull, got it. I'll step back a bit." He walks twelve feet to the southwest.

He hears Mara say, "You count. I'll pull on three."

Then he hears her say, "It already rang. Did you pull it?"

To himself he says, "Whoa, that was loud. Sorry, I thought we were pulling together. Let's pause a sec and listen, see if anything answers." Then, "I didn't pull it. I'd stepped back. Maybe the rope slipped when you had it?"

He says out loud, "Not me. I was stepping back. Maybe the rope slipped. If you still want to pull, I'll count you in on three. Or we can just listen a bit."

He walks back to her. He hears her say, "I'll pull it once. Count me in."

He says out loud, "One... two... three!"

He hears a loud bell ring.

Mara says, "That was it. Louder than what I thought I heard by the cabin. I'm going to listen a minute and see if anything answers."

He waits with her.

He inspects the bell. It is a bronze bell on a wooden frame, with a rope on the clapper.

He walks back to the stone circle. Then, in his own words: "I walk slowly clockwise around the stone ring, running my fingertips along each fitted block to feel for carvings, seams, looseness, or any odd warmth or coolness, and I peer into the joints for markings. After one full circuit, I step carefully into the grassy center, stand still, and listen."

"I'll jot a quick note, then check the trees to the north." He writes for about two minutes.

He hears Mara. "I'm here. Did you find anything on the stones?"

"I pause the note to answer her." This entry also stops partway through:

"Bell on NE headland: bronze, rope on clapper. Rang twice, deep, carries. No obvious mechanism beyond rope. Stone circle SE of bell: low fitted stones ~14 ft across, grass center, no markings or seams felt. Ocean to E (cliffs), pond W, forest N close by. Mara (20-ish) writing near bell. Next:"

He says out loud, "Hey, just a low ring of fitted stones, grass in the middle. No markings or seams that I could feel. Want to walk the forest edge with me and have a look?"

At the forest he hears Mara say, "No markings, then. I'll put my hand on one, and then I'll walk the forest edge with you."

He says out loud, "I'm at the forest edge, going to take a quick look just inside. Want to come?"

He tries to walk ten feet north into the trees. Something solid is in the way. He gets three feet. Meanwhile he sees Mara, thirty-nine feet away, do this. In her words: "I crouch and put my palm flat on the nearest fitted stone, feeling for carvings, seams, or any warmth that isn't just sun."

He inspects the forest. Dense trees with rough ground underneath. "Trees are tight here. I'll skirt west along the edge and look for a gap." He walks sixty feet west, then on to the pond.

He hears Mara shout: "The trees won't let me in. Something solid. I'm coming along the edge."

At the pond he says out loud, "I'm at the pond! Water's murky. I'm going to check the shoreline for tracks or a gap into the trees, meet me here?"

In his own words: "I move to the pond's edge and crouch, scanning the muddy shoreline for footprints, animal tracks, a worn path, or a thinner spot in the brush. I walk slowly clockwise around the near half of the pond, feeling for gaps toward the forest and checking for any fresh impressions in the mud."

He says out loud, "What are you looking at? I didn't find any clear tracks, just murky edges. See something I missed?"

Mara says, "I'm here. The trees stopped me, something solid, not just brush. Did you find tracks, or a way in?"

To himself he says, "No clear tracks, just soft edges, and I hit that same invisible wall in the trees. Let's try the bridge and follow the stream, might be an easier path." He does not say this out loud. He walks toward the wooden bridge.

He arrives at the bridge. Mara is now too far away to make out clearly, writing in a small notebook. "At the bridge now, peeking over to see how the stream runs and if there's a clear bank-side path." He starts to inspect the stream.

That is where his timeline ends, at thirty minutes, at the bridge. The last words he said out loud were at the pond, to Mara. He wrote two entries in his journal, and both stop mid-sentence.
